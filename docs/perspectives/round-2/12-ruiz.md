# Señora Ruiz, high school Spanish teacher and native speaker
**Verdict:** Would use with conditions. I would only use it as a first draft that I fully proofread myself.
**In one sentence:** The Spanish itself is mostly natural and well accented, but each of the three documents had a mistake or an ignored instruction that I would have caught only because I am a Spanish teacher, and the "side by side" bilingual worksheet is not really side by side.

## What I did
- Pages visited: home (/), tools list (/tools), tool forms for quiz, parent-email and worksheet, and the World Languages library page (/library?subject=world-languages). The library had "Nothing here yet" for World Languages, so I could not open a Spanish resource.
- Documents generated (3):
  1. 12-01-quiz.md: Spanish quiz, preterite vs. imperfect, 10 questions, entirely in Spanish.
  2. 12-02-parent-email.md: Family email in Spanish about a field trip to a Mexican restaurant.
  3. 12-03-worksheet.md: Ser vs. estar worksheet, English + Spanish (side by side), 10 questions.

## Problems found
1. **Worksheet answer key is wrong for question 7 (major).** Where: worksheet Part B, no. 7, "El concierto ___ en el parque, y ___ a las siete de la noche." Key says "está / es (location of the event; time of the event)". Events take ser for where they happen ("El concierto es en el parque"), so the correct answer is "es / es". A student who answers correctly would be marked wrong, and students would learn a false rule. Fix: correct the key and add the "events use ser" rule to the quick guide.
2. **Worksheet "side by side" is not side by side, and the Spanish sentences are just copied (major).** Where: Part A. Each item is printed twice in a row with identical Spanish ("Mi hermana (es / está) estudiante de secundaria." twice), and then a whole second "Español (Parte A)" copy follows. The exercise sentences are already Spanish, so no translation is needed, but the duplication wastes paper and looks broken. The directions and answer key are also repeated in full in both languages one after the other, not in two columns. Fix: put English and Spanish in two columns or paired lines, and never repeat target-language exercise items.
3. **Worksheet answer key is questionable on no. 4 and no. 5 (minor to major).** No. 5 "Las manzanas (son / están) rojas porque están maduras": the key says "son", but a native speaker says "están rojas" when ripe, and the sentence already uses "están maduras". No. 4 "Tú (eres / estás) muy simpático hoy": key says only "estás", but "eres" is also correct. Fix: make items with one clear answer, or accept both answers in the key.
4. **Worksheet teaches the "ser = permanent, estar = temporary" shortcut (minor).** Worked example says "material is permanent" and Part B no. 6 says "origin is permanent". Many teachers avoid this rule because it breaks on "está muerto" and "es la una". Fix: describe ser as identity, characteristics, origin, time and events, and estar as location and state.
5. **Quiz points do not add up (major).** Where: quiz header says "Puntuación: ______ / 30" and the key says "Puntuación total: 30 puntos". But Part A is 10, Part B is 14 (the key itself says 14) and Part C is 12, which is 36. Also Part B says "Cada pregunta vale 2 puntos", but no. 8 is worth 6 in the key. Fix: make the totals, the instructions and the key agree.
6. **Quiz question 3 has two correct answers (major).** "Anoche, Marta ___ un libro muy interesante que ___ en la biblioteca." Key says only "leyó / encontró", but "leía / encontró" is a perfectly good sentence (she was reading a book she had found). A good student would be marked wrong. Fix: reword so only one choice works, for example add "de repente" or "cuando sonó el timbre".
7. **Quiz key note for no. 6 is muddled (minor).** It says "sonó" is preterite, then says "sonaba" is also possible and "se prefiere el pretérito". That would confuse students and a sub reading the key. Fix: state the single clear reason (an interruption at a point in time is preterite).
8. **Quiz key heading is in English (minor).** "## Answer Key" in an all-Spanish quiz, while the worksheet correctly has "Clave de respuestas". Fix: use "Clave de respuestas" when the language is Spanish. Also "miraban la televisión" is regional; "veían la televisión" is more widely natural (minor).
9. **Family email ignored an instruction and contradicted it (major).** I wrote "bring $12 for lunch" and "permission slip due Wednesday". The email never mentions the $12. It tells families to send lunch from home, which is the opposite of what I said. It added a bracketed "[Nota para el docente: ...]" inside the parent-facing text, so it is easy to print by mistake. The date line is also "miércoles [fecha]" even though I gave Friday March 14 so Wednesday March 12 could be worked out. Fix: use details exactly as given (the $12, how to pay, Wednesday March 12) and never invent the opposite; put teacher notes outside the document.
10. **Library has nothing in World Languages (minor).** The page says "Nothing here yet for that combination", so a Spanish teacher has no ready-made starting point. It does point to "Make one now".

## What worked
- The Spanish was natural, with correct accents (pretérito, excursión, ¡Gracias!) and good punctuation including inverted marks.
- The register of the family email was right: "Estimadas familias", usted-form plural "firmen", "devuélvanlo", warm and polite, with a proper "a. m." and "p. m.".
- The quiz was a real grammar quiz with sensible items, a good reading passage, and explanations in the key. Most items (1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8) were sound.
- Language choices in the form (Spanish, English + Spanish) and an "In Spanish" button exist.

## What I could not check
- How anything looks when printed, on a phone, or what Print / Word / Edit do (including whether accents survive in Word).
- The about, privacy and terms pages, and anything in the library for Spanish.
- Whether the "In Spanish" revise button gives a better side-by-side result.
- Other Spanish dialect handling (Spain vs. Latin America); I only saw neutral Latin American Spanish.

## What I would say to a colleague about this site
It writes Spanish better than I expected and saves real time on the email and quiz, but do not hand anything straight to students. In my three documents there was a wrong answer key, a quiz with the wrong total and a two-answer question, and an email that ignored the lunch money. If you read the answer key carefully, it is worth using. The "side by side" bilingual option is not ready for me to put in front of a class.
