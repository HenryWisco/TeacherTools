# Mrs. Hartley, school board member elected on a parent-rights platform
**Verdict:** Would approve only with conditions. I would not want my district teachers pointed at it until the transparency gaps are closed.
**In one sentence:** The documents were calm and mostly sound, but nothing on a printed handout says an AI wrote it, nothing prompts a teacher to tell parents, and the sensitive-topic lessons contain a few factual slips that a parent could use against the school.

## Problems found
1. **Nothing on the document says an AI wrote it.** (blocker for my purposes) All three have only a Name/Date line at the top and no footer or note. If a teacher prints and hands out a puberty sheet, a parent cannot tell it was machine-written. The About page and Terms say documents are AI-written, but only the teacher sees those. Fix: a small, always-on line on every printout such as "Drafted with AI; reviewed by the teacher", ideally one the teacher cannot delete without a deliberate step.
2. **No prompt to inform parents on sensitive topics.** (major) The puberty plan has a "Privacy note" about not asking students for body details, and a question box, which I liked. It says nothing about checking district opt-out or parent-notification rules, or about sharing materials with families beforehand. Fix: for health, sexuality, race and history, and SEL topics, add a teacher note: "Check your district's parent-notification and opt-out policy before teaching. Consider sending materials home." Offer a one-click Family Email.
3. **Factual error in the slavery lesson timeline.** (major) The 1877 marker says "Federal troops withdraw... Southern states begin passing Black Codes and later Jim Crow laws." Black Codes began in 1865-66. The vocabulary table in the same document correctly says "after 1865". The document contradicts itself. Fix: place Black Codes at 1865-66 and Jim Crow after 1877.
4. **Invented-looking "quotations" from primary sources.** (major) Source A is given in quotation marks as a "1740 South Carolina Slave Code, excerpt paraphrased". The words are the AI's paraphrase, not the statute text, and presenting them in quotes risks being called fabricated evidence. Source B is a paraphrase of Douglass. Fix: either use only real, verifiable quotes, or label paraphrases clearly as "summary, not original wording" and tell the teacher to check the real text.
5. **Contradiction inside the puberty lesson.** (minor) The sort card says "Getting 9 to 11 hours of sleep" with a bracket correcting it to "9 to 12 hours". The answer key says 9 to 12. A teacher would have to catch and fix this before printing. Fix: make the generator state the number once and correctly.
6. **Puberty age ranges stated as fact with no source.** (minor) "about ages 8 and 13 for girls and about ages 9 and 14 for boys". These are plausible ranges but there is no citation (CDC, AAP). Parents will ask. Fix: name a source or soften to "typically".
7. **No standards cited, and the slavery lesson is not tied to the district's approved curriculum.** (minor, opinion) All three say "No standards were specified". A board member wants to know a lesson maps to adopted standards. Fix: a stronger nudge to enter standards.
8. **About page claims library items are "reviewed" without saying by whom.** (minor) /about: "ready-made resources that have been reviewed before being listed." I could not tell whether that is a human, a teacher, or another AI. Fix: say who reviews and how.
9. **Privacy page still has an unfinished note to the owner.** (minor, policy) "Site owner: review this page with a qualified adviser before launch, and add your business name and address." The same appears on Terms. Contact is only "the site owner", with no named person or address. Fix: fill in before launch.

## What worked
- The slavery lesson was honest and age-appropriate. It named the Thirteenth Amendment's punishment-for-crime exception and did not editorialize.
- The puberty lesson was restrained and factual, with a "talk to a trusted adult such as a parent" line and an anonymous question box. It did not stray into topics beyond the grade.
- The SEL lesson stuck to naming feelings and calm-down tools. No ideology.
- Answer keys were present and the timing in all three added up to the class length.
- The Privacy page is plain: no accounts, no student data, adults only.
- About page is upfront that "AI can still make mistakes".

## What I could not check
Look and layout, phone view, what Print/Word do, whether any footer is added when printing, and the admin pages (I was told not to use them).

## What I would say to a colleague about this site
It is better behaved than I expected, and it was honest about being AI. But the honesty is on the web pages, not on the handout that reaches a child's desk. Before any teacher in our district uses it for health or history, I would want a visible AI label on every printout, a reminder to follow parent-notification rules, and a human check of the facts. The slavery timeline error shows why that check cannot be skipped.
