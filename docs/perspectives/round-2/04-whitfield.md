# Mr. Whitfield, AP English Literature teacher

**Verdict:** Would not use it for literature as it stands. The rubric tool is usable with edits; the Hamlet discussion questions contained errors that would embarrass me in front of seniors.

**In one sentence:** It put a line from *Macbeth* in Hamlet's mouth, placed Claudius's prayer couplet in the wrong scene, and ignored most of what I asked for in two of three documents, so I cannot trust it with a text I have not just reread myself.

## What I did

Pages visited: home (/), /tools, /about, /privacy, /terms, the English library (/library?subject=ela), the library resource "Literary Analysis Essay Rubric" (grade 12), the Discussion Questions tool page, and the forms for Discussion Questions, Rubric and Reading Passage.

Documents generated (three):

1. `04-01-discussion-questions.md` - Hamlet, Act 3, Socratic seminar. I asked for exactly 10 questions, each quoting a line from Act 3 with act, scene, line and speaker.
2. `04-02-rubric.md` - Timed literary argument essay on Morrison's *Beloved* (fractured chronology and memory), 5 levels, four criteria I named, out of 20, descriptors specific to the novel.
3. `04-03-reading-passage.md` - Original fiction, about 800 words, grade 12, for AP prose analysis. I asked for 8 AP-style multiple-choice questions with options A-E citing paragraph numbers, one essay prompt, and a key with rationales.

All three came back in 10 to 18 seconds.

## Problems found

### 1. A Macbeth line attributed to Hamlet (blocker)
Where: `04-01-discussion-questions.md`, question 7.
What I saw: "Hamlet asks, 'Is this a dagger which I see before me?' in his soliloquy (Act 2, Scene 1, not Act 3)."
That is Macbeth, Act 2 Scene 1. It is not in *Hamlet* at all. The question then moves on to a real Hamlet line, but the false sentence is printed on a student handout.
Fix: Never state a quotation and attribution unless it is certain; if the tool is not sure, leave it out. A question should contain only the line being discussed.

### 2. Claudius's couplet placed in the wrong scene, with an invented line beside it (blocker)
Where: `04-01-discussion-questions.md`, question 2.
What I saw: "Claudius says, 'O, 'tis too much' when he hears of the fencing match (not in Act 3), but in Act 3, Scene 1, he admits, 'My words fly up, my thoughts remain below...' (lines 97-98)."
"My words fly up" closes the prayer scene, Act 3 Scene 3, not Scene 1. I know of no line "O, 'tis too much" spoken by Claudius about the fencing match. The question itself refers to "his prayer", so the document contradicts its own citation.
Fix: Correct the scene to 3.3 and remove the invented lead-in.

### 3. The tool's own second thoughts were printed on the handout (major)
Where: `04-01-discussion-questions.md`, questions 2, 4 and 7.
What I saw: "(not in Act 3)", "(Act 2, Scene 1, not Act 3). For Act 3, consider..." and, in question 4, an Act 2 line ("I'll loose my daughter to him") dragged in before the Act 3 line. I asked for Act 3 only. It reads as though the machine began a wrong question, noticed, and kept both halves.
Fix: Check each question against the request before showing it; drop anything outside the act asked for, and never print self-corrections.

### 4. Asked for exactly 10 questions, got 15, and the page still says ten (major)
Where: `04-01-discussion-questions.md`, overview and numbering.
What I saw: "These ten questions are for an AP English Literature Socratic seminar" followed by questions numbered 1 to 15. Question 15 quotes no line at all, which I had required of every question.
Fix: Honour a number given in the "Anything else" box, or add a "How many questions" field to the Discussion Questions form (it has none). The introduction must match the actual count.

### 5. "Speaking as Hamlet" tacked onto every question (major)
Where: `04-01-discussion-questions.md`, questions 1 to 15.
What I saw: "Speaking as Hamlet, what does the word 'nunnery' carry in this line..." and "Speaking as Gertrude, is she offering a judgment..." Fourteen times. I asked it to name the speaker; it seems to have turned that into a stock phrase that tells students to answer in character, which is not what the questions want. Some also misdescribe the line: Gertrude does not "ask" that the lady protests too much, and Claudius does not "ask" "Love? His affections do not that way tend"; both are statements. Hamlet is called "alone" in the prayer scene while Claudius kneels in front of him.
Fix: Name the speaker once, in the lead-in ("Hamlet tells Ophelia..."), and remove the "Speaking as..." phrase.

### 6. Reading passage ignored the question format I asked for (major)
Where: `04-03-reading-passage.md`, "Comprehension Questions" and "Answer Key".
What I asked for: 8 multiple-choice questions, five options A-E, plus an essay prompt. What I got: seven short-answer questions and one multiple-choice item with four options (a-d), no essay prompt. The one multiple-choice item is vocabulary at middle-school level for a grade 12 request: "'faint' most nearly mean? a. bright b. light or barely visible c. fainted d. ancient".
Fix: Let the "Anything else" instructions override the default question set, or add a "question style" choice (short answer / multiple choice / AP-style) to the Reading Passage form. Pitch distractors to the grade chosen.

### 7. Question and answer key contradict the passage (major)
Where: `04-03-reading-passage.md`, question 7 and its key.
What I saw: Question 7 says Nora leaves "without the shelf's contents". The passage ends: "carried the tin box to the car, and did not go back for the shelf." She takes the contents and leaves the shelf. The key then says "Nora's decision to leave the shelf and act on the word 'Call'". She does not act on it; her father is dead and no call is made. Question 7 also announces "the passage's theme about communication between parents and children", handing students the reading they are supposed to arrive at.
Fix: Check every question and key answer against the passage text. Do not state the theme in the question stem.

### 8. Passage is longer than asked, and has small inconsistencies (minor)
Where: `04-03-reading-passage.md`, paragraphs 1, 3, 6, 9.
What I saw: I chose "About 800 words"; I counted about 930. The workshop is rented ("The lease ended in nine days. The landlord...") but at sixteen her father "had only nodded and gone back to the garage", and paragraph 9 speaks of "the house she had not yet cleaned" as if the workshop were part of it. Paragraph 6: "nothing at all, only a single underlined word" says nothing and something in one breath.
Fix: Keep within roughly 10 percent of the chosen length; read the passage once for continuity of setting.

### 9. Beloved rubric: mostly sound, with slips about the novel and one poor fit for a timed essay (minor)
Where: `04-02-rubric.md`, Thesis and Evidence rows, checklist.
What I saw: "States a arguable, specific claim" (typo in the top-level descriptor). "Sethe's repeated returns to 124" (she lives there; she does not keep returning). "the opening of Chapter 1" (the novel has no numbered chapters). "the 'Rememory' passage in which the past persists in the house" (in that passage Sethe is telling Denver that Sweet Home, the farm, is still out there waiting; it is not about 124). For a timed essay, it demands quotations "accurately cited" and a checklist item "quoted accurately with citations"; students writing closed-book cannot cite pages. The points arithmetic is right (4 criteria x 5 = 20) but it is stated three times, and the lowest possible score is 4, with no line for a blank or off-topic paper.
Fix: Correct the four textual slips; for timed essays ask for "specific, accurate reference to the text" in place of citations; state the total once; add a 0 for no response.

### 10. Line-number note is misleading (minor)
Where: `04-01-discussion-questions.md`, overview.
What I saw: "Line numbers follow the standard Folger-style through-numbering used in most modern editions." The numbers given (nunnery at 3.1.121, "Now might I do it pat" at 3.3.73) match the Arden/Riverside tradition, not my Folger, where those lines fall about ten lines later; and Folger does not use through-numbering. The advice to check against your own edition is sensible; the claim before it is not.
Fix: Say only "Line numbers vary by edition; check against yours," or let the teacher name the edition.

### 11. The legal pages still carry notes to the owner (minor)
Where: /terms and /privacy, bottom of each page.
What I saw: "Site owner: review this page with a qualified adviser before launch, and add your business name and address." Contact is given only as "the site owner", with no address or email.
Fix: Finish these pages and give a real contact before launch.

## What worked

- Most of the Hamlet quotations were real, correctly worded and in the right scene: the nunnery line, "the mirror up to nature", "Now might I do it pat", "enseamed bed", "counterfeit presentment", "Pray can I not", "almost blunted purpose". Several questions were good ones I would use: the one on "counterfeit", the one on "protest", and question 12 (is Claudius's failure one of faith, will or honesty).
- The Beloved rubric honoured every setting: five levels, my four criteria in my order, total of 20. The descriptors actually mention the novel, and the Sophistication row (the infanticide told out of sequence as "both protective and accusatory") is better than I expected. The weak-commentary example ("Sethe remembers the milk and this shows she is sad") is exactly the kind of sentence I see.
- The library's grade 12 Literary Analysis Essay Rubric is clean and sensible. "If an essay falls between two levels, score the lower level and name what would move it up" is good practice, and the summary-versus-analysis table is useful.
- The fiction passage is competent, better than I expected: concrete detail (screws "sorted by size in his square capitals"), a controlled ending. It is also built from stock parts (tin box of letters, stopped calendar, unfinished note), and paragraph 7 explains its own meaning ("understood that she had been reading the wrong document") after I asked for restraint. That is taste, not error.
- The site is honest that it is AI and tells teachers to check. About, Terms and the footer all say so. No sign-up, and the privacy page is plain about what goes to the AI provider.

## What I could not check

- How anything looks, prints, or exports to Word or Google Docs.
- Whether the rubric table fits on one landscape page with five columns of long descriptors.
- Whether the "Revise" buttons would repair the problems above (for instance "cut to 10 questions").
- Whether a pasted scene in the "Your own text" box would make the quotations and line numbers reliable. I suspect it would help; I did not spend a document on it.
- The "My documents" and Support pages.

## What I would say to a colleague about this site

For a rubric skeleton, fine: it will save you twenty minutes and you will fix a few phrases. For anything that quotes a literary text, do not print it until you have checked every line against the book. In fifteen questions on one act of the most-taught play in English it gave me a line from another tragedy, an invented line, and a wrong scene, and it left its own corrections on the page. A first-year teacher in a hurry would not catch those, and a sharp senior would. It also does what it wants rather than what you ask when it comes to how many questions and what kind. I came in expecting bland; what I found was mostly fluent and sometimes wrong, which is harder to spot.
