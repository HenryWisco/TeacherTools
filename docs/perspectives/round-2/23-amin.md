# Dr. Amin, school board member and pediatrician focused on equity

**Verdict:** Would approve with conditions.

**In one sentence:** The site tries harder than most to reach students with disabilities and families who speak other languages, and the differentiation output was useful and free to carry out, but translations go out with no warning to have them checked, a leveled answer key had a real error, and the library offers nothing for English learners, bilingual families or special education yet.

## What I did

Pages visited: home (/), /tools, /tools/parent-email, /tools/accommodations, /library, /library?subject=esl, a library search for "spanish", the library resource /library/short-vowel-cvc-words-lesson-plan-grade-1, /my, /about, /privacy, /terms. I also looked at the form fields for Family Email, Differentiation Ideas, IEP Goal Ideas, Text Leveler and Reading Passage.

Documents generated (3):

1. **23-01-parent-email.md**: Family Email about a free vision and hearing screening for 2nd graders, with an opt-out form and help finding low-cost eye exams. Language typed as "English + Somali", a language that is not in the dropdown on other tools.
2. **23-02-accommodations.md**: Differentiation Ideas for a grade 5 lesson on the heart. Class of 28 in a high-poverty school with no devices and no budget, including a blind student who reads braille, a Deaf student with an ASL interpreter, two students with dyslexia, one with ADHD, a wheelchair user who cannot write by hand, and three newcomer students who speak Arabic.
3. **23-03-reading-passage.md**: Reading Passage, grade 3 health, "Why sleep matters", three levels, English + Spanish side by side, with a request not to blame families who work night shifts or share beds, and 4 questions.

## Problems found

### 1. Translations go out with no warning to have them checked, and the Somali one has visible errors (major)
- **Where:** Family Email output (23-01-parent-email.md). The same applies to every tool's Language option.
- **What I saw:** The Somali half of a health consent notice came with no note that it was machine-translated. Some words look wrong. The sign-off "Warm regards" became "Salaan qaboow leh", and "qabow" means *cold*. "Sealed envelope" became "jaakad xidhan", where "jaakad" means jacket; envelope is "baqshad". "School nurse" became "dhakhtarka caafimaadka dugsiga", roughly "school health doctor", which misstates who families will deal with. The sentence meant to say there is no cost ("Waalidiinta ma kharash ayaa ka dhici doonta") reads as garbled. The English half was accurate and complete.
- **Why it matters:** An opt-out notice about a child's health screening is exactly where a district must give families an accurate translation. A teacher who cannot read Somali has no way to know it needs checking.
- **Fix:** Put a short line at the top of every translated document, outside the printable text or as a removable note: "Machine translation. Have a fluent speaker or your district's interpreter check it before sending, especially for health, safety, consent or discipline messages." Consider showing the warning more strongly for languages outside the main list.

### 2. Answer key gives the wrong sentence for "why sleep is not extra" (major, real error)
- **Where:** 23-03-reading-passage.md, main passage Question 4, Level 2 Question 4, and the answer key.
- **What I saw:** The question asks students to "Find one sentence from the passage that explains why the author says sleep is not extra." The key says the answer is "Sleep is not extra." That is the claim, not the explanation. The explanation is the next sentence: "It is part of how your body and brain grow strong…". The main key contradicts itself ("…from 'It is part of how…' or the sentence 'Sleep is not extra.' (The exact sentence in the passage is 'Sleep is not extra.')"), and the Level 2 key simply says "Sleep is not extra." The Spanish key has the same mistake.
- **Fix:** The key should give "It is part of how your body and brain grow strong, so it deserves a regular place in your day." (Spanish: "Es parte de cómo tu cuerpo y tu cerebro se fortalecen al crecer…").

### 3. Level 3 answers and question quote text that isn't in the passage Level 3 students read (minor, real error)
- **Where:** 23-03-reading-passage.md, Level 3 Questions 2 and 4 and their answer key.
- **What I saw:** Level 3 uses the main passage. Its Question 2 quotes "not anyone's fault", but the main passage says "None of these things is anyone's fault". The words "This is not anyone's fault" appear only in the Level 1 short version. The Q4 key offers "Families can help with small changes…" and in Spanish "Las familias pueden ayudar con pequeños cambios…" as example answers. Both sentences are also only in the Level 1 version, and the Spanish one uses "ayudar", not the "ayuda/ayudan" the question asks for.
- **Fix:** Quote and cite only from the passage each level actually reads.

### 4. "Side by side" bilingual output was English first and Spanish after, and leveled pages depend on earlier pages (minor; partly could not check)
- **Where:** 23-03-reading-passage.md.
- **What I saw:** I chose "English + Spanish (side by side)", but the text came as "Part A: English" followed by "Part B: Español" for each level. The printed layout may differ, but I could not see it. Levels 2 and 3 say "Use the same passage as in the main reading above" and don't include the passage. A teacher who prints only the Level 3 pages hands out questions with no passage. Level 2 also repeats the main questions word for word. The Level 1 "worked example" answers Question 1 word for word, so it gives that answer away.
- **Fix:** Make each level a complete handout with its own passage. If "side by side" really means two columns, check that the printout shows them that way. Otherwise rename the option "English then Spanish".

### 5. Differentiation tool puts students with sensory and physical disabilities in the "need more support" group (major, equity)
- **Where:** 23-02-accommodations.md, section "Students Who Need More Support".
- **What I saw:** "Students in this group are the blind student who reads braille, the Deaf student who uses an ASL interpreter, the two students with dyslexia, and the wheelchair user who cannot write by hand." A blind, Deaf or wheelchair-using student may be working at or above grade level and needs *access*, not easier work. The handout then offers "Level 1: Full Support" fill-in-the-blank questions, which nudges teachers to lower expectations for these students. The tool also never says to check the student's IEP or 504 plan, or to ask the student and family what already works.
- **Fix:** Separate "access" supports (braille, interpreter, scribe, speech-to-text, tactile diagrams) from "academic level" supports. Add one line saying to follow the student's IEP/504 plan and involve the special education team.

### 6. The differentiated handouts refer to a picture that isn't there; newcomer support stops at a word list (minor)
- **Where:** 23-02-accommodations.md, "Task at Three Levels" and "English Learners".
- **What I saw:** Level 1 says "Use the word list and the picture below", but there is no picture or word list in the document. For the three Arabic-speaking newcomers, the tool tells the teacher to make an Arabic word list "with a bilingual staff member or a trusted online dictionary", even though the site has an Arabic language option and could have drafted it. It suggests pairing newcomers with "a peer who speaks English well" rather than an Arabic-speaking peer, if there is one. The sheet also has a student "Name / Date" line at the top even though it is a teacher planning document.
- **Fix:** Include the word list (with the translation when a language is selected) and a simple outline diagram, or remove "below". Suggest a same-language peer first.

### 7. Library has nothing for English learners, bilingual families or special education, and no large print (major, missing feature)
- **Where:** /library?subject=esl and a library search for "spanish".
- **What I saw:** The ESL / ELL filter shows "Nothing here yet for that combination." A search for "spanish" returns nothing. The library's type list has no Family Email, Differentiation or IEP items. Library resource pages offer "Print / save PDF", "Word" and "Copy for Google Docs" but no "Large print" button, though generated documents have one. Every ready-made resource I saw was English-only.
- **Fix:** Before launch, seed the library with some ESL resources and some bilingual (at least Spanish) family letters, and add the Large print button to library resource pages.

### 8. Language list is short and inconsistent between tools (minor)
- **Where:** Language field on every tool, compared with /tools/parent-email.
- **What I saw:** Most tools offer only 10 fixed choices: English, Spanish, English + Spanish, French, Portuguese, Chinese (Simplified), Vietnamese, Arabic, Tagalog, Haitian Creole. Common family languages in many US districts are missing, such as Somali, Russian, Korean, Ukrainian, Dari/Pashto, Hmong, Nepali and Swahili. The Family Email tool alone takes free text, which is how Somali worked. The quick-revise buttons offer only "In Spanish". The Family Email revise buttons also show "Harder" and "More questions", which make no sense for a letter home.
- **Fix:** Use the free-text language box (or an "Other language…" choice) on every tool, together with the translation warning in item 1. Show revise buttons that fit each tool.

### 9. Privacy page has no real contact and still shows placeholder notes; disability details aren't named as personal information (major for board approval)
- **Where:** /privacy and /terms; /tools/accommodations.
- **What I saw:** "Questions or removal requests: the site owner." There is no email address or form. Both pages still say "Site owner: review this page with a qualified adviser before launch…". The Differentiation tool's main boxes carry no privacy reminder; the only one, "leave out student names", sits under the optional text box. In a small school, "one blind student who reads braille" can identify a child as surely as a name can.
- **Fix:** Add a working contact address and the business name, remove the placeholder notes, and next to the Differentiation and IEP boxes add: "Describe needs in general terms; avoid details that could identify a student, especially in a small school." The privacy page should also say what the AI provider does with the text.

## What worked

- **The differentiation output was practical and truly low-cost.** It suggested raised-line tactile diagrams made by pressing a ballpoint pen into heavy paper, giving the interpreter vocabulary the day before, captions on any video, numbered matching cards made from scrap paper for the student who cannot write, and chunked reading with a checklist card for ADHD. The pulse-math challenge was correct (beats per minute × 1,440 = beats per day). The heart facts and answer keys were correct, and the 70–100 resting heart rate range for a 10-year-old is reasonable.
- **It respected my "don't blame families" instruction.** The sleep passage handled night-shift work and shared beds gently: "None of these things is anyone's fault." The fact that children this age need 9–12 hours of sleep matches pediatric guidance. The Spanish was natural and accurate as far as I could tell.
- **The English family email was clear and complete.** It covered every point I gave (no cost, opt-out by Friday, bring glasses, sealed envelope, nurse helps uninsured families), with placeholders instead of invented dates.
- **Equity choices in the policies.** No accounts, no student data, ads kept away from the family message, IEP and differentiation tools, a "Large print" button on generated documents, a "For English learners" revise button, and a skip-to-content link.

## What I could not check

- What "Large print", "Print", "Word" and "Share" actually produce, and whether the side-by-side bilingual layout appears as two columns when printed.
- How Arabic (right-to-left) text prints.
- Colour contrast, screen-reader use, keyboard navigation and phone layout.
- The quality of the Haitian Creole, Vietnamese or other translations. I can only partly judge the Somali one.
- The IEP Goal Ideas tool output (I looked at the form only).

## What I would say to a colleague about this site

This is better than I expected on equity. The differentiation ideas assumed a school with no money, which is the real situation for many of our buildings, and they gave concrete help for blind, Deaf and physically disabled students rather than generic advice. But I would not let a teacher send a translated health or consent notice from it unchecked: nothing on the page says the translation might be wrong, and the Somali sample had clear mistakes. I'd also want the tool to stop grouping disabled students with "needs easier work", and the library to have something for English learners and bilingual families before launch. With a translation warning, a fixed answer key and a real privacy contact, I would be comfortable telling teachers it's a reasonable free option, as long as they read everything first.
