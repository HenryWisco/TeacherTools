# Lesson Plan: Identifying and Managing Feelings

**Name:** ______________________  **Date:** ____________

## Lesson Overview

- **Grade:** 3
- **Subject:** Social-Emotional Learning
- **Class length:** 30 minutes
- **Standards:** None provided. Align to your district or state SEL competencies if required.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:

- I can name at least five different feelings.
- I can tell the difference between a feeling and what I do about it.
- I can name one calming strategy I can use when I have a big feeling.

## Materials

- Whiteboard or chart paper and markers
- A "Feelings Faces" chart with five to six simple faces (happy, sad, angry, worried, excited, calm), drawn or printed
- One index card per student
- A small bell, chime or timer
- Student copies of the Feelings Check-In sheet (below), one per student
- Pencils

## Key Vocabulary

- **Feeling:** What we notice inside our body and mind, such as happy, sad or worried.
- **Emotion:** Another word for a feeling, often one that is strong, such as anger or joy.
- **Identify:** To name or recognize something.
- **Manage:** To handle a feeling in a healthy way so it does not control our actions.
- **Calm-down strategy:** A safe action that helps our body and mind slow down, such as deep breathing.

## Lesson Sequence

| Step | Time | Activity |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 5 min | Feelings Faces game |
| Direct Instruction | 7 min | Feelings vs. actions; body clues; calm-down tools |
| Guided Practice | 7 min | Class "Feeling Detective" practice |
| Independent Practice | 7 min | Feelings Check-In sheet |
| Closure | 4 min | Exit card and share-out |
| **Total** | **30 min** | |

### 1. Hook (5 minutes)

Say: "Today we are going to be feelings detectives. Let's start with a game. I will make a face, and when I do, show me the feeling with your own face. Don't say it out loud yet."

Make these faces, one at a time, pausing for a few seconds each: happy, sad, angry, worried, surprised, calm.

After each face, ask: "What feeling do you see? Show me your feeling face."

Then ask: "How did you know what I was feeling?" Take two or three answers, such as "Your eyebrows were down," or "Your mouth was turned down."

### 2. Direct Instruction (7 minutes)

Write on the board: **Feeling** and **Action**.

Say: "A feeling is what is happening inside us. An action is what we do. Feelings are not bad. Every feeling is okay. But some actions can hurt us or others, so we learn to manage what we do with our feelings."

Give examples and ask students to sort them with a thumbs up (feeling) or thumbs down (action), or by calling "Feeling" or "Action":

1. "I feel nervous." (Feeling)
2. "I slam my door." (Action)
3. "I feel proud." (Feeling)
4. "I stop talking and walk away." (Action)

Then teach body clues. Say: "Feelings often show up in our bodies. When I feel angry, my face might get hot, my hands might clench, or my heart might beat fast. Where do you feel worry? Some people feel it in their stomach."

Introduce three calm-down tools and demonstrate each one:
- **Belly breath:** Breathe in through your nose for 3 counts, hold for 1, breathe out through your mouth for 3.
- **Shake it out:** Gently shake your hands and arms for 5 seconds.
- **Name it:** Quietly say, "I feel ___ right now."

### 3. Guided Practice (7 minutes)

Say: "Let's be feelings detectives together. I'll read a short story, and you'll help me find the feeling, the body clue, and a calm-down choice."

Read this story aloud:

> Jordan wanted a turn on the swing. Another child was already on it. Jordan's face felt hot, and their fists were tight.

Ask:
1. "What is Jordan's feeling?" (Frustrated, or angry. Accept reasonable answers.)
2. "What body clue tells us?" (Hot face, tight fists.)
3. "What is one calm-down choice Jordan could use?" (Belly breath, taking a breath, asking for a turn calmly, waiting.)

Record answers on the board under three headings: **Feeling**, **Body Clue**, **Calm Choice**.

Then ask the class: "Which feeling is hardest to name? Why?" Let two or three students respond.

### 4. Independent Practice (7 minutes)

Hand out the Feelings Check-In sheet. Read the directions aloud:

"Read each sentence. Write the feeling in the blank. Then circle the calm-down choice that would help. Draw a face to show the feeling."

### 5. Closure (4 minutes)

Give each student an index card. Say: "On your card, write or draw one feeling you had this week and one calm-down tool that helped or could help."

Ask three or four volunteers to share. Close with: "Feelings are information. We get to choose what we do with them."

## Differentiation

**For students who need support:**
- Provide the Feelings Faces chart at each desk as a reference.
- Offer a word bank on the Check-In sheet: happy, sad, angry, worried, excited, calm, nervous, proud, frustrated.
- Allow students to point to a face or draw a feeling instead of writing.
- Pair students with a partner for the guided practice.

**For students ready for more:**
- Ask: "Can two feelings happen at the same time? Give an example." (For example, proud and nervous before a performance.)
- Have students write a fourth situation for the Check-In sheet and name a feeling and a calm choice for it.
- Ask: "Which calm-down tool would work best in a classroom? Why?"

## Formative Assessment: Exit Card Check

Use the index card from the closure step, or ask students to hold up fingers after the independent practice.

| Question | Expected Answer | Evidence of Understanding |
|---|---|---|
| Name a feeling. | Any accurate feeling word (happy, sad, angry, worried, etc.) | Student names one feeling word |
| Is "I slam my door" a feeling or an action? | Action | Student correctly sorts the example |
| What is one calm-down tool? | Belly breath, shake it out, or name it | Student names one tool |

**Scoring guide:**
- **3 of 3 correct:** Ready to move on. Continue practicing with new situations.
- **2 of 3 correct:** Mostly secure. Review the feeling-versus-action distinction in a small group.
- **0–1 correct:** Needs support. Work with the student one-on-one using the Feelings Faces chart and a short practice story.
