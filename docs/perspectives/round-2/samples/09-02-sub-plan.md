# Substitute Plan: Beginning Concert Band Rehearsal

**Name:** ______________________  **Date:** ____________

## Notes for the Substitute

Thank you for covering this class. You do not need to read music or play an instrument. The students are experienced enough to run their own warm-up with a student leader, and the plan below tells you exactly what to say and do.

**Expected routines**
- Students enter, take out their instruments, and begin quiet personal warm-up (about 2 minutes) after you say "Instruments out, quiet warm-up."
- The student leader is [student leader name, or "chosen by the class at the start of the period"]. Their job is to count off the warm-up and cue the group. Give them the leader sheet at the start of class.
- Instrument cases go under chairs or along the wall at [case storage location]. Instruments stay in hands or on laps unless the activity says otherwise.
- Stands and chairs are in [seating chart location]. Check the seating chart before class starts.

**What to collect**
- Attendance using [attendance method].
- The Written Reflection sheets (Part C) at the end of class. Place them in [turn-in location].

**If students bring instruments they were not told to bring, or have a broken or missing instrument**
- Students without an instrument may do the listening and written activities in Parts B and C. They should sit with the group and follow along.
- A student with a broken or damaged instrument should not play it. Ask them to note the problem on their reflection sheet and tell the band teacher in the next note.
- If a student brings an instrument that is not theirs, return it to the owner or to [loaner or lost-and-found location]. Do not allow it to be played.
- Do not let students handle reeds, mouthpieces, or cleaning rods for another person's instrument.

**If students finish early**
- Students may work on the Instrument Care Quiz on the next page. Answers are in the Answer Key.
- Students may begin the Listening Reflection (Part B) early, if they have finished Part A.

**Safety and behavior**
- Keep instruments away from the edge of the table and off the floor where people walk.
- Never allow students to blow into another student's instrument or share mouthpieces.
- If an accident or injury happens, call the office at [office extension or number].

## Part A: Warm-Up and Rehearsal (30 minutes)

### Timed Schedule

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0:00 to 0:05 | Attendance and instruments out |
| 0:05 to 0:10 | Student-led breathing and tuning warm-up |
| 0:10 to 0:20 | Student-led rehearsal of the class piece, [piece title] |
| 0:20 to 0:25 | Instrument care review (Part A, Section 3) |
| 0:25 to 0:30 | Pack up and instruments into cases |

### Section 1: Opening (0:00 to 0:05)

Read this aloud:

"Good morning. My name is [your name], and I am covering for [teacher name] today. Your band teacher left instructions for your rehearsal. Please take out your instruments and sit quietly. Your student leader, [student leader name], will run the warm-up."

Give the student leader the **Student Leader Sheet** below.

### Section 2: Student-Led Warm-Up and Rehearsal (0:05 to 0:20)

**Student Leader Sheet**

Read this aloud to the student leader:

"You will lead the warm-up and the rehearsal today. Follow the steps on your sheet. If anything goes wrong, stop, wait for quiet, and ask the group to try again."

1. Start with long tones. Ask the group to hold one note for four counts, then breathe. Repeat three times.
2. Lead a tuning check. Ask each section to play a concert B-flat (or the note your teacher has written on the board). Listen, then ask the group to adjust.
3. Lead the warm-up exercise on the board, [exercise name or page number]. Count off, then count off again at a slower tempo if needed.
4. Lead the class piece, [piece title], from measure [start measure] to measure [end measure]. Stop after each section to ask the group what they noticed.
5. Ask the group to play one time through the full piece if there is time.

Students should not play during the explanation. After each step, the student leader should say "Quiet, please" and wait.

**Adult supervision during rehearsal:** Stand near the back of the room. Watch for students who are not playing or who are off-task. You do not need to correct the music. If a student needs help with their instrument, ask them to raise a hand and wait for the band teacher.

### Section 3: Instrument Care (0:20 to 0:25)

Read this aloud:

"Before you pack up, I want you to look over your instrument. Tell the person next to you one thing you do to keep your instrument clean, and one thing you should not do."

Then give students one minute to talk with a partner. After one minute, say:

"Thank you. Please make sure your instrument is dry. Check for moisture, especially if you played a woodwind or brass instrument. Place the mouthpiece in its case pocket and close the case."

### Section 4: Pack Up (0:25 to 0:30)

Read this aloud:

"Now we will pack up. Stand up, check for your belongings, and place your instrument in its case. Keep your instrument in the case until you leave the room. When you are ready, leave quietly."

Students who finish early may begin Part B.

## Answer Key

1. Moisture left inside an instrument can cause rust, mold, and damage to pads, corks, and metal parts. Drying it protects the instrument and keeps it playing well.
2. Store it in its case, in a safe place away from heat, cold, and direct sunlight.
3. Never share or use another student's mouthpiece, and never blow into another student's instrument. Sharing can spread germs and damage the instrument.
4. Do not play it. Tell your band teacher right away so it can be repaired.

**Reflection answers are personal and will vary.** Accept any reasonable response that answers the question completely in full sentences.
