# Lesson Plan: Photosynthesis and Cellular Respiration

**Name:** ______________________  **Date:** ____________

**Grade:** 10  **Subject:** Science  **Class length:** 50 minutes

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, I can:

- Write the word equation and the balanced chemical equation for photosynthesis.
- Write the word equation and the balanced chemical equation for cellular respiration.
- Explain how the products of one process are the reactants of the other.
- Identify where each process occurs in the cell (chloroplast and mitochondrion).

## Standards

No standards were specified. Align this lesson to your district or state life science standards on energy flow and matter cycling in ecosystems.

## Materials

- Whiteboard or projector with the equations in Part 2 written out
- Student handout (the Formative Assessment at the end of this plan) printed one per student
- Colored pens or pencils (two colors)
- Optional: a short video on chloroplasts and mitochondria, if available at your school

## Key Vocabulary

- **Photosynthesis:** The process by which plants and some other organisms use light energy to make glucose from carbon dioxide and water, releasing oxygen.
- **Cellular respiration:** The process by which cells break down glucose with oxygen to release energy stored in ATP, producing carbon dioxide and water.
- **Chloroplast:** The organelle where photosynthesis takes place. It contains chlorophyll.
- **Mitochondrion:** The organelle where most of the energy from cellular respiration is released. Plural: mitochondria.
- **Chlorophyll:** The green pigment that absorbs light energy.
- **Glucose:** A simple sugar (C6H12O6) that stores chemical energy.
- **ATP (adenosine triphosphate):** The molecule cells use to power their work.
- **Reactant:** A substance that is used up in a chemical reaction.
- **Product:** A substance that is formed in a chemical reaction.

## Lesson Sequence (50 minutes)

| Time | Step | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 min | Hook | Ask the class the question below and take three quick answers. |
| 5–18 min | Direct instruction | Teach the two processes and their equations. |
| 18–28 min | Guided practice | Balance equations together and map the cycle. |
| 28–40 min | Independent practice | Students complete the card sort and the cycle diagram. |
| 40–47 min | Formative assessment | Students complete the exit questions on the handout. |
| 47–50 min | Closure | Review the key idea and collect exit slips. |

### Step 1: Hook (5 minutes)

Write this on the board and ask the class:

"A tree weighs many kilograms, yet it grows mostly from air and water. Where does the mass come from?"

Take three answers without judging them. Tell students the answer is partly the air (carbon dioxide) and partly the water, and that today they will find out how.

### Step 2: Direct Instruction (13 minutes)

**Photosynthesis (about 6 minutes)**

Write on the board:

Word equation: carbon dioxide + water + light energy → glucose + oxygen

Balanced equation: 6CO2 + 6H2O + light energy → C6H12O6 + 6O2

Explain:
- Photosynthesis happens in the chloroplasts, using chlorophyll to capture light.
- Light energy is stored as chemical energy in glucose.
- Oxygen is released as a by-product.

Ask: "Count the carbon atoms on each side. Why do we need 6 CO2?" (Each glucose molecule has 6 carbon atoms, so 6 carbon dioxide molecules are needed.)

**Cellular respiration (about 7 minutes)**

Write on the board:

Word equation: glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water + energy (ATP)

Balanced equation: C6H12O6 + 6O2 → 6CO2 + 6H2O + energy (ATP)

Explain:
- Respiration happens in the mitochondria, in both plants and animals.
- Cells break glucose down to release energy in ATP.
- Oxygen is needed to release most of the energy.

Point out the link: the products of photosynthesis (glucose and oxygen) are the reactants of respiration, and the products of respiration (carbon dioxide and water) are the reactants of photosynthesis.

### Step 3: Guided Practice (10 minutes)

Work through these with the class on the board. Have students respond on their own paper before you reveal each answer.

1. Balance this equation: _ H2 + _ O2 → _ H2O. (Answer: 2, 1, 2)
2. Balance this equation: _ CO2 + _ H2O → _ C6H12O6 + _ O2. (Answer: 6, 6, 1, 6)
3. Which organelle does photosynthesis happen in? (Chloroplast)
4. Which process releases oxygen? (Photosynthesis)
5. Which process needs oxygen? (Cellular respiration)

Draw a cycle on the board with four arrows labeled: glucose, oxygen, carbon dioxide, water. Ask a volunteer to trace the cycle and explain it in one sentence.

### Step 4: Independent Practice (12 minutes)

Students work on the handout, Part A (card sort) and Part B (cycle diagram).

**Part A: Card sort.** Students sort these statements into two columns labeled Photosynthesis and Cellular respiration:

- Takes in carbon dioxide (Photosynthesis)
- Releases carbon dioxide (Cellular respiration)
- Uses light energy (Photosynthesis)
- Releases energy as ATP (Cellular respiration)
- Happens in chloroplasts (Photosynthesis)
- Happens in mitochondria (Cellular respiration)
- Produces oxygen (Photosynthesis)
- Uses oxygen (Cellular respiration)

**Part B: Cycle diagram.** Students draw the cycle with two colors, one for each process, and label the four substances.

Circulate and check diagrams. Common error: drawing oxygen as a product of respiration. Correct it by asking, "Which process makes oxygen?"

### Step 5: Formative Assessment (7 minutes)

Students complete the exit questions in the Formative Assessment section below.

### Step 6: Closure (3 minutes)

Ask: "In one sentence, how are photosynthesis and cellular respiration connected?" Take one or two answers. Collect exit slips at the door.

## Differentiation

**For students who need support:**
- Provide a word bank with the reactants and products of each process.
- Give the balanced equations with blanks to fill in, rather than requiring students to balance from scratch.
- Pair these students with a partner for the card sort and allow them to talk through answers before writing.
- Provide a labeled diagram of a plant cell showing the chloroplast and mitochondrion.

**For students ready for more:**
- Ask: "Why does the carbon in glucose come from carbon dioxide, not from the soil?" (Plants build their sugars from carbon in the air, through photosynthesis. Soil supplies water and minerals, not the carbon backbone of sugar.)
- Ask: "Why can't a plant survive in complete darkness for long, even though it has mitochondria?" (It can still respire, using stored glucose. Without light, it cannot make new glucose, so its stored energy runs out.)
- Have students calculate the mass of oxygen released when 180 g of glucose is produced. (Molar mass of glucose is 180 g/mol, and 6O2 is 192 g, so 180 g of glucose releases 192 g of oxygen.)

## Answer Key

1. 6CO2 + 6H2O + light energy → C6H12O6 + 6O2
2. In the mitochondria.
3. Photosynthesis. It uses light energy to split water and release oxygen as a by-product.
4. Glucose and oxygen from photosynthesis are the reactants of cellular respiration. Respiration breaks down the glucose with oxygen to release energy as ATP, producing carbon dioxide and water.

**Scoring guide:** 4 points for a fully correct answer to each question. Award 2 points for a partly correct answer. For Question 4, full credit requires naming both glucose and oxygen and stating that respiration releases energy.
