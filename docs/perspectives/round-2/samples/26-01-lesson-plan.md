# Comparing Fractions with the Same Numerator or the Same Denominator

**Name:** ______________________  **Date:** ____________

## 1. Candidate Information

Teacher Candidate: [Name]  Cooperating Teacher: [Name]  University Supervisor: [Name]
School: [School Name]  Grade: 3  Date of Lesson: [Date]  Number of Students: 22

## 2. Central Focus

Students will reason about the size of fractions that share a numerator or a denominator. This lesson builds on their work with unit fractions and fractions on a number line, and it prepares them to compare any two fractions in later grades. Students will use fraction strips and number lines to show why 1/4 is larger than 1/8, and why 2/5 is larger than 1/5.

## 3. Standards

**CCSS.MATH.CONTENT.3.NF.A.3.D:** Compare two fractions with the same numerator or the same denominator by reasoning about their size. Recognize that comparisons are valid only when the two fractions refer to the same whole. Record the results of comparisons with the symbols >, =, or <, and justify the conclusions, for example, by using a visual fraction model.

## 4. Learning Objectives

**ABCD objective:** Given pairs of fractions with the same numerator or the same denominator, 22 third-grade students will compare the fractions using fraction strips or a number line and write the correct symbol (>, =, or <) with a one-sentence reason, with at least 80% accuracy on the end-of-lesson check.

**Student-friendly "I can" statements:**
- I can compare two fractions that have the same bottom number.
- I can compare two fractions that have the same top number.
- I can explain my answer using a fraction strip or a number line.

## 5. Academic Language

**a. Language Function:** Compare

**b. Vocabulary:**
- **Numerator:** The top number of a fraction. It tells how many parts we have.
- **Denominator:** The bottom number of a fraction. It tells how many equal parts the whole is split into.
- **Unit fraction:** A fraction with a numerator of 1, such as 1/3.
- **Equal parts:** Parts that are the same size.
- **Whole:** The complete amount that is split into parts.
- **Greater than (>):** Bigger than.
- **Less than (<):** Smaller than.
- **Equivalent:** Equal in value, even if the numbers look different.

**c. Syntax and Discourse:** Students compare using a sentence frame.
- Sentence frame: "____/____ is greater than (or less than) ____/____ because ____."
- Example for same denominator: "3/8 is greater than 2/8 because 3 eighths is more eighths than 2 eighths."
- Example for same numerator: "1/3 is greater than 1/6 because thirds are bigger pieces than sixths when the whole is the same."

**d. Language Supports:**
- Post the vocabulary words with pictures of fraction strips.
- Model the sentence frame on the board before students use it.
- Allow students to point to the fraction strip and say the answer before writing it.
- Provide a word bank with the symbols >, <, and = and their names.
- Use the phrase "the same whole" repeatedly so the key idea is familiar.

## 6. Prior Knowledge and Assets

**What students already know:**
- Unit fractions, such as 1/2, 1/3, and 1/4.
- How to locate fractions on a number line from 0 to 1.
- How to name the numerator and denominator of a fraction.
- Basic use of the symbols > and <.

**Assets to draw on:**
- Invite Spanish-speaking students to share how they say "parts," "half," or "bigger" in Spanish. Write these on the board next to the English words.
- Ask students to share everyday examples of sharing food or pizza with family members, which connects fractions to home experience.

## 7. Materials and Technology

- Fraction strips (one set per pair of students), labeled 1 whole, 1/2, 1/3, 1/4, 1/6, 1/8
- Mini whiteboards and dry-erase markers, one per student
- Large class number line from 0 to 1 (drawn on chart paper or the board)
- Projector or board for the "I do" examples
- Printed sheet: Practice Problems (see Independent Practice)
- Exit ticket slips, one per student
- Sentence frame chart, posted in the room

## 8. Instructional Procedures

Total: 45 minutes

**a. Anticipatory Set / Hook (5 minutes)**
Hold up two paper pizzas of the same size. Cut one into 4 equal slices and the other into 8 equal slices. Ask: "If you wanted the bigger piece, which pizza would you rather have a slice from, the one cut into 4 or the one cut into 8?" Take 2 or 3 responses. Then say: "Today we will learn how to decide which fraction is bigger without guessing."

**b. Direct Instruction: I Do (10 minutes)**

*Part 1: Same denominator (4 minutes)*
Put up fraction strips for 2/5 and 4/5 on the board. Say: "Both fractions are cut into 5 equal parts. The denominator is the same, so the parts are the same size. Count the parts: 4 is more than 2, so 4/5 is greater than 2/5. Write 2/5 < 4/5."
Model the sentence frame: "4/5 is greater than 2/5 because 4 fifths is more fifths than 2 fifths."

*Part 2: Same numerator (4 minutes)*
Put up fraction strips for 1/3 and 1/6. Say: "Both fractions have a 1 on top, so each one is one piece. But thirds are cut into 3 pieces and sixths into 6 pieces. When you split the same whole into fewer pieces, each piece is bigger. So 1/3 is bigger than 1/6. Write 1/6 < 1/3."
Ask students to watch: "Does the bottom number tell you if the piece is big or small?"

*Part 3: The same whole rule (2 minutes)*
Say: "We can only compare fractions when they are parts of the same whole. A third of a small pizza is not the same as a third of a big pizza."

**c. Guided Practice: We Do (12 minutes)**

*Number line (4 minutes)*
Draw a number line from 0 to 1 on the board and mark 1/4 and 3/4. Ask: "Which fraction is closer to 1?" Students show on their whiteboards: 1/4 < 3/4 or 3/4 > 1/4. Check all whiteboards together.

*Fraction strip pairs (6 minutes)*
Students work with partners using fraction strips. Call out these pairs one at a time. Partners compare, then show their symbol on the whiteboard:
1. 3/8 and 5/8
2. 1/2 and 1/4
3. 2/6 and 2/3
4. 1/5 and 1/8

Ask partners to say the sentence frame aloud before writing the symbol.

*Quick check (2 minutes)*
Ask: "Why is 1/2 bigger than 1/4? Turn to your partner and explain." Listen for "fewer pieces means bigger pieces."

**d. Independent Practice: You Do (13 minutes)**

Students complete the Practice Problems on the printed sheet. Students who finish early move to the extension problems.

**Practice Problems**
1. Write >, <, or =. Use fraction strips if you need help.
   a. 2/4 ___ 3/4
   b. 1/3 ___ 1/5
   c. 5/6 ___ 1/6
   d. 1/8 ___ 1/4
   e. 3/5 ___ 4/5
   f. 2/3 ___ 2/6

2. Choose one problem from Question 1. Write one sentence that explains your answer.
   _________________________________________________________________
   _________________________________________________________________

**Extension (for students who finish early)**
3. Name two different fractions that are both greater than 1/3 and have a denominator of 6.
4. Explain why 1/2 and 2/4 are equal, and use fraction strips to show it.

**e. Closure (5 minutes)**
Ask students to complete the exit ticket. Then ask: "What is one rule you can use when two fractions have the same bottom number? What about the same top number?" Take 2 or 3 responses and record the rules on the chart: "Same denominator: more parts means bigger." "Same numerator: fewer total pieces means bigger pieces."

## 9. Differentiation

**a. Students with IEPs (reading)**
- Read all directions aloud and provide a printed copy with key words highlighted.
- Allow students to answer Question 2 orally or by drawing instead of writing.
- Provide a sentence starter they can copy, such as "____ is greater than ____ because ____."
- Let students use fraction strips for every problem, not only when they ask.
- Check in with each student during independent practice.

**b. English Language Learners (Spanish)**
- Preview the vocabulary with pictures before the lesson. Include Spanish words: numerador (numerator), denominador (denominator), mayor que (greater than), menor que (less than).
- Use the sentence frame on the board and at each table.
- Accept answers that use gestures, pointing, or a drawing before a written sentence.
- Pair ELL students with a supportive partner who speaks English clearly.

**c. Advanced Learners**
- Give the extension problems (Questions 3 and 4).
- Ask: "Can you find a fraction that is between 1/4 and 1/2? How do you know?"
- Ask students to create a comparison problem for a classmate, with an answer key.

## 10. Assessment

**a. Formative Assessment (during the lesson)**
- Whiteboard checks during the number line and fraction strip pairs. Observe whether students write the correct symbol.
- Partner explanation during the quick check. Listen for the key ideas: same denominator means same-size pieces, and fewer pieces means bigger pieces.
- Observe whether students use the sentence frame during partner work.

**b. Summative / End-of-Lesson Assessment: Exit Ticket**
Write these on the exit ticket:
1. Write >, <, or =: 3/6 ___ 5/6
2. Write >, <, or =: 1/4 ___ 1/2
3. Explain your answer to Question 2 in one sentence.

**Expected answers:**
1. 3/6 < 5/6 (same denominator; 3 sixths is less than 5 sixths)
2. 1/4 < 1/2 (same numerator; fourths are smaller pieces than halves)
3. Sample: "1/2 is greater than 1/4 because halves are bigger pieces, since the whole is split into fewer parts." Accept any sentence that names the fewer-pieces or bigger-piece idea.

**c. Evaluation Criteria**
- **Meets the objective:** The student writes the correct symbol for both questions and gives a one-sentence reason that names the denominator or numerator idea (bigger pieces or more parts) for Question 3.
- **Approaching:** The student writes at least one correct symbol, or the reason is incomplete or uses a fraction model without words.
- **Not yet:** The student writes fewer than one correct symbol. Reteach using fraction strips in a small group the next day.
- Target: at least 80% of students (18 of 22) meet the objective.

## 11. Classroom Management Considerations

- **Transitions:** Use a signal, such as a hand clap pattern, to move from direct instruction to partner work. Practice the signal before the lesson starts.
- **Grouping:** Pair students in advance. Place each IEP student with a patient, supportive partner. Place ELL students with a partner who speaks English clearly.
- **Materials distribution:** Place a fraction strip set on each table before class. Hand out mini whiteboards at the start of the guided practice. Give the practice sheets face down so students do not start early.
- **Expectations:** Establish "whiteboard down, eyes up" for teacher directions. Tell students they will show their answers all at once on a count of three.
- **Fraction strip care:** Assign one student per table as materials man
