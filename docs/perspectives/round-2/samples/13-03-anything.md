## Answer Key

### Version A

1. Algae and pondweed are the producers. They make their own food using sunlight, so they are the base of the food chain.
2. Primary consumer: water snails or tadpoles (both eat algae and pondweed). Secondary consumer: dragonfly nymphs or small fish (both eat water snails or tadpoles). Either pair of answers is acceptable, as long as each consumer's food is named correctly.
3. Bacteria and fungi are decomposers because they break down dead plants and animals into simpler materials. This returns nutrients to the water and mud so producers can use them again.
4. 20,000 kcal x 0.10 = 2,000 kcal reach the primary consumers.
5. 2,000 kcal x 0.10 = 200 kcal reach the secondary consumers.
6. 200 kcal x 0.10 = 20 kcal reach the tertiary consumer (largemouth bass).
7. Each level passes only about 10 percent of its energy to the next level, and the rest is used for life processes or lost as heat. After four or five transfers, so little energy is left that there is not enough to support another level. Sample full-credit response: "Because only about 10 percent of energy moves up each level, the amount drops quickly. By the fifth level, very little energy is left to support another predator."
8. If the algae and pondweed were lost, the producers would no longer capture sunlight energy. The water snails and tadpoles would lose their food source and their numbers would fall. With fewer snails and tadpoles, dragonfly nymphs and small fish would have less food, and largemouth bass would then lose food too. Energy would stop entering the food web, so the whole pond community would decline over time.

### Version B

1. The consumers are the water snails, tadpoles, dragonfly nymphs, small fish and largemouth bass. They cannot make their own food, so they eat other organisms.
2. Producer: algae or pondweed, which make food from sunlight. Decomposer: bacteria or fungi, which break down dead plants and animals in the mud and return nutrients to the pond.
3. The largemouth bass is a tertiary consumer because it is at the third level of consumers. For example, in the chain algae → water snail → dragonfly nymph → largemouth bass, the bass is the fourth organism and the third consumer. Any valid chain with the bass as the third consumer is acceptable.
4. 20,000 kcal x 0.10 = 2,000 kcal reach the primary consumers.
5. 2,000 kcal x 0.10 = 200 kcal reach the secondary consumers.
6. 200 kcal x 0.10 = 20 kcal reach the tertiary consumer (largemouth bass).
7. Only about 10 percent of energy passes from one level to the next, so each level supports far fewer organisms than the level below. Sample full-credit response: "Producers hold 20,000 kcal, but a top predator receives only about 20 kcal. Because of this, there must be many more producers than predators to supply enough energy."
8. If the water snails were removed, tadpoles would lose a food source, so they might increase at first because fewer snails compete with them for algae. Algae would likely increase because fewer snails eat them. The largemouth bass would have less food from the dragonfly nymphs and small fish, which depend on snails less directly, so the bass would eventually be affected as energy flow through the pond changes. Overall, the energy in the pond would move along different paths, and the community would become unbalanced until it adjusted.
