# Differentiation Ideas: Honeybees and Pollination (Grade 4 Science)

**Name:** ______________________  **Date:** ____________

## Purpose

This document adapts the Grade 4 science task (read a one-page article on honeybees and pollination, then write a five-sentence paragraph explaining why bees matter) for a self-contained special education class of eight students in grades 3 to 5. Reading levels range from pre-primer to grade 2. Two students are non-speaking and use AAC devices, one student has low vision, and several students have fine motor difficulties and cannot yet write a full paragraph by hand.

The adaptations keep the science goal the same for every student: explain that bees help plants make seeds and fruit by moving pollen between flowers, and that this matters for food and for other living things. What changes is how students access the article, how they show their understanding, and how much support they receive.

## Students who need more support

1. **Read the article aloud in short chunks, with a picture for each.** Before the lesson, copy the article onto cards, one short paragraph per card, and pair each card with a simple photo or drawing (bee on flower, pollen on bee's legs, apple tree, fruit). A paraprofessional reads each card aloud and points to the picture. Students place the cards in order on the table.

2. **Pre-teach five key words with real objects or pictures.** Use: flower, pollen, bee, seed, fruit. Show a real flower or a picture, hold up a sticky note of yellow dust to represent pollen, and have students repeat each word. Keep the word wall at eye level on a single sheet.

3. **Give a sentence frame sheet with picture prompts.** Students complete frames such as "Bees help ______ make ______" by choosing from picture cards (apple, flower, seed, honey) or by pointing on an AAC page. Students with reading levels at pre-primer level do not write; they pick and arrange.

4. **Use a three-row "Bees Help" organizer.** Row 1: bee picture. Row 2: a flower picture with a movement arrow. Row 3: a fruit picture. Students match or point. This replaces the five-sentence paragraph for students who are not yet writing sentences.

5. **Offer a fine-motor-friendly response.** Students who struggle with handwriting can stamp, use stickers, cut and glue picture strips, or type on a keyboard with a large-key or word-prediction setting. Students may also use a whiteboard with a marker grip or a slant board.

## English learners

6. **Use a bilingual picture glossary.** Students who speak a home language other than English receive the five key words with pictures and a translation in their home language. Students may point to the picture and say or sign the home-language word first, then the English word.

7. **Let students respond in their first language or with AAC before English.** Students may draw or select answers on the AAC device, then add English words afterward. Credit the science reasoning, not the English form.

8. **Provide a "bee sentence" starter bank.** Offer three ready-made sentence starters: "Bees help plants by ______." "Without bees, ______." "Bees matter because ______." Students choose one and finish it with a picture or a word.

## Students with attention or executive-function needs

9. **Break the task into four visible steps on a strip of paper.** Step 1: Listen to one part of the article. Step 2: Point to the picture that matches it. Step 3: Choose one reason bees matter. Step 4: Share your answer. Check off each step with a marker as it is completed.

10. **Limit the reading to one short section at a time.** Read paragraph one, pause for a task (match picture to word), then read paragraph two. Keep timers visible for each section, set to three to five minutes.

11. **Use a visual "what's next" card.** Show the sequence of activities with pictures so students can see when the reading ends and the response begins.

12. **Allow movement breaks between sections.** For example, students act out a bee moving from flower to flower between sections, then return to the table.

## Students who need a challenge

No student in this class is ready for grade-level extension. For students who are working above their current support level, use the following instead:

13. **Add one "why" question.** Ask students who are reading at grade 2 to choose two pictures and say or write one reason the first picture helps the second (for example, "Bees carry pollen so flowers make seeds"). Offer this only after the core task is complete.

14. **Have students sort and explain.** Give a set of picture cards (bee, flower, apple, honey, cow, car) and ask students to sort them into "bees help" and "bees do not help" with a one-word reason.

15. **Give a choice of response.** Students who are ready may write a two-sentence answer instead of the full paragraph, or may build a three-picture chain: bee, pollen, fruit.

## Three levels of the task

Each level targets the same science idea: bees move pollen, which helps plants make seeds and fruit.

### Level A: Picture and point (pre-primer to early reading)

| Step | What the student does | Response |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Listen to the article in four picture cards | Put cards in order |
| 2 | Find the key words | Point to bee, flower, fruit |
| 3 | Complete the frame: "Bees help ______ make ______" | Choose picture cards or AAC buttons |
| 4 | Share the answer | Point, sign, or use AAC |

### Level B: Word and sentence (grade 1 to early grade 2)

| Step | What the student does | Response |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Read the article in short sections with picture support | Paraprofessional reads aloud |
| 2 | Match key words to pictures | Label the "Bees Help" organizer |
| 3 | Finish a sentence starter from the bank | Write or type one or two words, or use AAC |
| 4 | Write or dictate three sentences | Use stickers, stamps, typing, or dictation |

Sentence starters for Level B: "Bees help plants by ______." "Without bees, ______." "Bees matter because ______."

### Level C: Short paragraph (grade 2 reading level)

| Step | What the student does | Response |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Read the article with a partner or with audio support | Partner or audio |
| 2 | Identify two reasons bees matter | Write or type on an organizer |
| 3 | Write a three- to five-sentence paragraph | Typed, dictated, or written with adaptive grip |
| 4 | Read the paragraph back or share on AAC | Self-check with the rubric below |

Level C rubric (use for students at this level):

| Criteria | Yes | Not yet |
|---|---|---|
| The paragraph says bees move pollen between flowers | | |
| The paragraph says this helps plants make seeds or fruit | | |
| The paragraph includes at least one reason bees matter to people or animals | | |
| The paragraph has three to five sentences | | |

Accessibility notes for all levels: provide the article in large print or high-contrast format for the student with low vision, read it aloud, and ensure AAC devices have a page with bee, flower, pollen, seed, fruit, and matter vocabulary loaded before the lesson.

## Answer Key

For this task, responses are open-ended and are judged against the science idea, not a single correct wording.

Acceptable science ideas include:
- Bees carry pollen from one flower to another.
- Moving pollen helps plants make seeds and fruit.
- Fruit and seeds help people and animals eat and grow new plants.
- Without bees, some plants would make less food.

Level A responses are correct if the student points to or selects the bee, flower, and fruit pictures in a sensible order and completes the frame with flower and fruit or seed.

Level B responses are correct if the sentence names bees, pollen or flowers, and plants or fruit or seeds.

Level C responses are correct if the paragraph meets the four rubric criteria.
