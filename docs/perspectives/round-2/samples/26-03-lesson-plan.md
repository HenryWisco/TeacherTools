# Comparing Fractions with the Same Numerator or Same Denominator

**Name:** ______________________  **Date:** ____________

## Candidate Information

Teacher Candidate: [Candidate Name]  Cooperating Teacher: [Name]  University Supervisor: [Name]
School: [School Name]  Grade: 3  Date of Lesson: [Date]  Number of Students: 22

## Central Focus

Students compare fractions that share a numerator or a denominator by reasoning about the size of the equal parts and the number of parts. This builds the foundation for comparing any two fractions and for understanding fractions as numbers on a number line. The lesson comes after students have learned unit fractions and equal-part fraction models.

## Standards

**CCSS.MATH.CONTENT.3.NF.A.3.D:** Compare two fractions with the same numerator or the same denominator by reasoning about their size. Recognize that comparisons are valid only when the two fractions refer to the same whole. Record the results of comparisons with the symbols >, =, or <, and justify the conclusions, for example, by using a visual fraction model.

## Learning Objective(s)

Given fraction strips and a set of paired fractions, each student will correctly compare at least 4 out of 5 pairs that share a numerator or a denominator, using >, =, or <, and will give a reason for each answer by pointing to the fraction model or the size of the parts.

**I can** compare fractions that have the same top number or the same bottom number, and explain why.

## Academic Language

**a. Language Function:** Compare

**b. Vocabulary**

- **Numerator:** the top number of a fraction; it tells how many parts we have.
- **Denominator:** the bottom number of a fraction; it tells how many equal parts the whole is split into.
- **Equal parts:** parts that are the same size.
- **Greater than (>):** the fraction on the left is bigger.
- **Less than (<):** the fraction on the left is smaller.
- **Equal to (=):** the two fractions are the same size.
- **Whole:** the complete shape or amount that is split into parts.

**c. Syntax and Discourse**

Students use this sentence frame to justify comparisons:

"___ is greater than / less than / equal to ___ because ___."

Example: "1/4 is greater than 1/8 because fourths are bigger pieces than eighths, so one fourth is more."

Example: "3/6 is less than 5/6 because both have 6 equal parts, and 3 parts is fewer than 5 parts."

**d. Language Supports**

- Post the vocabulary words with pictures and fraction strip examples.
- Model the sentence frame aloud before students use it, and keep it displayed.
- Allow students to point to or touch a fraction strip while speaking.
- Allow students to answer with a symbol, a drawing, or a single word first, then build to a full sentence.
- Pair English learners with a partner who speaks a little more English, and let them discuss in Spanish before sharing in English.

## Prior Knowledge and Assets

- Students know unit fractions (1/2, 1/3, 1/4, 1/6, 1/8) and can name the numerator and denominator.
- Students have used fraction strips to show equal parts of a whole.
- Students can already tell that a pizza cut into 4 slices has bigger slices than one cut into 8 slices. Use this everyday idea as a bridge.
- Students bring experience sharing food, snacks, and classroom materials. Invite them to talk about times they split something fairly, which connects to equal parts.

## Materials and Technology

- Fraction strips in halves, thirds, fourths, sixths, and eighths (one set per pair of students)
- Whiteboards or paper and dry-erase markers for each student
- Projector or chart paper with the sample problems and the sentence frame
- Printed comparison cards (one set per pair): see the Independent Practice section
- Comparison symbol cards (>, <, =) for the class
- Pencils

## Instructional Procedures

Total: 45 minutes

**a. Anticipatory Set / Hook (5 minutes)**

Hold up two paper pizzas of the same size. Cut one into 4 slices and one into 8 slices. Ask: "If I take one slice from each pizza, which slice is bigger? How do you know?" Take 2 or 3 responses. Then say, "Today we will learn how to compare fractions like these without a pizza."

**b. Direct Instruction, I Do (10 minutes)**

1. Write 1/4 and 1/8 on the board. Say: "Both fractions have a 1 on top, so each one is one piece. The bottom number tells how many equal parts the whole is cut into. Fourths are cut into 4 parts, eighths into 8 parts. More parts means smaller pieces. So 1/4 is greater than 1/8."
2. Place a one-whole fraction strip for fourths above a strip for eighths. Show that one fourth covers more length than one eighth. Write: 1/4 > 1/8.
3. Write 3/6 and 5/6. Say: "Both fractions have 6 equal parts, so the pieces are the same size. We just count how many pieces. 5 pieces is more than 3 pieces, so 5/6 is greater than 3/6."
4. Show the sentence frame and model one full sentence: "1/4 is greater than 1/8 because fourths are bigger pieces than eighths."
5. Explain the rule clearly: when the denominators are the same, compare the numerators. When the numerators are the same, compare the denominators, and the fraction with the smaller denominator is greater.

**c. Guided Practice, We Do (12 minutes)**

Students work in pairs with fraction strips. Read each problem aloud, have students build the fractions with strips, and have them decide on the symbol together. Then have them use the sentence frame to justify.

1. Compare 2/3 and 2/8. (Answer: 2/3 > 2/8)
2. Compare 4/6 and 2/6. (Answer: 4/6 > 2/6)
3. Compare 1/2 and 1/6. (Answer: 1/2 > 1/6)
4. Compare 5/8 and 3/8. (Answer: 5/8 > 3/8)
5. Compare 1/4 and 1/4. (Answer: 1/4 = 1/4)

After problem 5, ask: "Why are these equal even though they are written the same way?" Expected answer: they are the same fraction, so they are the same size.

Circulate and listen for students using the frame. Ask follow-up questions such as, "How do you know which piece is bigger?" or "What do the bottom numbers tell you?"

**d. Independent Practice, You Do (13 minutes)**

Students complete the comparison sheet on their own, using fraction strips to check. Pairs who finish early move to the extension problems.

**Comparison Sheet**

Write >, <, or = in each box. Then write one reason.

1. 1/3 [ ] 1/6
2. 3/4 [ ] 1/4
3. 2/8 [ ] 2/6
4. 5/6 [ ] 5/8
5. 1/2 [ ] 1/2
6. 3/8 [ ] 7/8
7. 4/6 [ ] 4/8
8. 1/4 [ ] 1/3

Reason for number 3: ______________________________________________

Reason for number 8: ______________________________________________

**Expected answers:**

1. 1/3 > 1/6 (thirds are bigger pieces than sixths)
2. 3/4 > 1/4 (same denominator, 3 pieces is more than 1 piece)
3. 2/8 < 2/6 (both have 2 parts, but eighths are smaller than sixths)
4. 5/6 > 5/8 (same numerator, sixths are bigger pieces than eighths)
5. 1/2 = 1/2 (same fraction)
6. 3/8 < 7/8 (same denominator, 3 pieces is fewer than 7 pieces)
7. 4/6 > 4/8 (same numerator, sixths are bigger pieces than eighths)
8. 1/4 < 1/3 (same numerator, thirds are bigger pieces than fourths)

**e. Closure (5 minutes)**

Ask students to hold up a symbol card (>, <, or =) as you read these comparisons aloud: 1/2 and 1/8; 2/6 and 5/6; 3/4 and 3/4. Then ask one student to explain using the sentence frame. End with: "Today you learned that when fractions have the same top number or the same bottom number, you can compare them by thinking about the size of the pieces and how many there are."

## Differentiation

**a. Students with IEPs (reading)**

- Read all problems and directions aloud, and provide a printed copy with the vocabulary words in bold.
- Allow students to show answers by placing fraction strips or by circling the correct symbol instead of writing a full reason.
- Give a sentence starter with the blank filled in for the first problem, then fade the support.
- Check in during independent work to confirm understanding of directions.

**b. English Learners (Spanish)**

- Provide a bilingual vocabulary card: numerador (numerator), denominador (denominator), partes iguales (equal parts), mayor que (greater than), menor que (less than), igual a (equal to).
- Allow discussion and planning in Spanish, then sentences in English using the frame.
- Use gestures and fraction strips to show meaning alongside the words.

**c. Advanced Learners**

- Extension problems: Put these fractions in order from least to greatest: 1/2, 1/3, 1/6, 1/8. Then do the same for 3/4, 3/6, 3/8.
- Challenge: Write one comparison that is true for same-denominator fractions and one that is true for same-numerator fractions, and explain each.

## Assessment

**a. Formative Assessment (during the lesson)**

- Thumbs or symbol cards during Direct Instruction and Closure to check understanding in real time.
- Teacher observation of pairs during Guided Practice, listening for correct use of the sentence frame.
- Quick check: Ask "Which is bigger, 1/3 or 1/6? Why?" and note which students answer correctly.

**b. Summative / End-of-Lesson Assessment**

The Comparison Sheet (Independent Practice) serves as the end-of-lesson check.

**c. Evaluation Criteria**

- **Meets objective:** Student correctly compares at least 4 of 5 pairs that share a numerator or denominator, uses the correct symbol, and gives a reason that refers to the size of the parts or the number of parts.
- **Approaching:** Student compares 2 or 3 pairs correctly, or gives symbols without reasons.
- **Not yet:** Student compares fewer than 2 pairs correctly, or consistently compares numerators and denominators as if they were whole numbers (for example, saying 1/8 is greater than 1/4 because 8 is greater than 4).

## Classroom Management Considerations

- Give fraction strips to pairs at the start, placed in labeled bags on each table, to avoid mid-lesson distribution.
- Pair students in advance, placing English learners with supportive partners and mixing pairs for variety.
- Use a clear signal (for example, raised hand and counting down from 3) to bring the class back to attention.
- Set expectations before pair work: one person speaks while the other checks with the strips, then switch.
- Keep transitions brief by timing each segment on the board.
