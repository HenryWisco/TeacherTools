# Multiplying and Dividing Decimals: Rural Life Word Problems

## Level 1: More support

**Name:** ______________________  **Date:** ____________

Tip: Multiplying decimals: multiply as whole numbers, then count the decimal places in both factors and put that many in the answer. Example: 2.4 x 3 = 7.2 (one decimal place in the answer).

Word bank: total, each, altogether, split, equal parts, per, cost

1. A rancher hauls water in a tank. Each trip brings 2.5 gallons. Hannah makes 4 trips. How many gallons does she haul in all?

2. A sheep weighs 65.5 pounds. The farmer buys 3 sheep of the same weight. What is the total weight of the 3 sheep?

3. The school bus drives 6.4 miles each morning. How many miles does it drive in 5 mornings?

4. Corn costs $0.75 per bag at the county fair. Marcus buys 6 bags. How much does he spend?

5. A farmer has 12.6 pounds of wool. She packs it into 3 equal bags. How many pounds go in each bag?

6. Ten gallons of water fill tanks for the animals. Each tank holds 0.8 gallon. How many tanks can be filled?

7. The fair sells ribbon at $2.40 per yard. Lena buys 2.5 yards. Multiply to find the cost. (Hint: 2.40 x 2.5 is the same as 240 x 25, then move the decimal point 3 places to the left.)

8. A ranch family drives 84.6 miles to town and back. They make 2 round trips in one week. How many miles do they drive in all? First find the miles in one round trip, then multiply.

Use this sentence starter for your answer on problem 8: "The family drives _____ miles in one round trip, so in 2 round trips they drive _____ miles."

## Level 3: Challenge

**Name:** ______________________  **Date:** ____________

1. Hannah hauls water to 3 tanks. Each tank holds 4.5 gallons. She hauls 2 full loads to each tank. Each load is 4.5 gallons. Her truck can carry 12.5 gallons per trip. How many trips does she need to deliver all the water? Explain your reasoning.

2. A sheep weighs 65.5 pounds. A farmer says that 3 sheep weigh more than 200 pounds. Is she correct? Show how you know without finding the exact total, then check with multiplication.

3. The school bus route is 6.4 miles each morning. The bus runs 5 mornings a week for 36 weeks. How many miles does it drive in the school year? Write your answer as a decimal, then explain how the decimal point moved in your work.

4. Marcus buys corn at $0.75 per bag. He wants to spend exactly $10.00. How many bags can he buy, and how much change will he get? Explain why you divide by 0.75 here.

5. A farmer packs 12.6 pounds of wool into bags that each hold 0.7 pound. How many bags does she fill? Explain how dividing by a decimal is like dividing by a whole number after scaling both numbers.

6. Lena buys ribbon at $2.40 per yard. She has $18.00 to spend. Write and solve an equation to find how many yards she can buy, including a short explanation of whether your answer should be rounded down.

7. The ranch family drives 84.6 miles per round trip. Their truck gets 18.0 miles per gallon. Find the gallons of gas for 2 round trips. Show each step and check whether your answer is reasonable.

8. Create your own problem about the county fair using a price per item with two decimal places and a quantity with one decimal place. Solve it and explain how you know your decimal point is in the right place.

## Answer Key

**Level 1**

1. 2.5 x 4 = 10. Hannah hauls **10 gallons**. (25 x 4 = 100, one decimal place, so 10.0 = 10.)
2. 65.5 x 3: 655 x 3 = 1965, one decimal place, so **196.5 pounds**. (Check: 65 x 3 = 195, plus 0.5 x 3 = 1.5, total 196.5.)
3. 6.4 x 5: 64 x 5 = 320, one decimal place, so **32 miles**. (Check: 6 x 5 = 30, 0.4 x 5 = 2.)
4. 0.75 x 6: 75 x 6 = 450, two decimal places, so 4.50. Marcus spends **$4.50**.
5. 12.6 / 3 = **4.2 pounds** per bag. (3 x 4.2 = 12.6.)
6. 10 / 0.8: 100 / 8 = 12.5, so **12 full tanks** can be filled (12 x 0.8 = 9.6 gallons; 13 tanks would need 10.4 gallons).
7. 2.40 x 2.5: 240 x 25 = 6000, move 3 places left = 6.000. Lena pays **$6.00**. (Check: 2.4 x 2.5 = 6.)
8. One round trip: 84.6 x 2 = 169.2 miles. Hmm, the question asks for 2 round trips. Calculation: 84.6 x 2 = **169.2 miles** in one week. (Round trip is already the 84.6 total, so 2 round trips = 169.2 miles.)

**Level 2**

1. 2.5 x 4 = **10 gallons**. (The 150-gallon tank is not needed for this total.)
2. 65.5 x 3 = **196.5 pounds**.
3. 6.4 x 5 = **32 miles**.
4. 0.75 x 6 = **$4.50**.
5. 12.6 / 3 = **4.2 pounds** per bag.
6. 10 / 0.8 = 12.5, so **12 full tanks**.
7. 2.40 x 2.5 = **$6.00**.
8. 84.6 x 2 = **169.2 miles**.
Multi-step: 4.5 x 3 = 13.5 gallons per round of loads. Hannah hauls 2 loads to each of 3 tanks, so 6 loads. 4.5 x 6 = **27 gallons** altogether. (Check: 13.5 x 2 = 27.)

**Level 3**

1. Total water: 4.5 x 2 = 9 gallons per tank, 9 x 3 = 27 gallons total. 27 / 12.5 = 2.16, so **3 trips** (2 full trips carry 25 gallons, which is not enough). Reasoning: the answer must round up because a partial trip still counts.
2. **No.** 65.5 x 3 = 196.5, which is less than 200. Estimate: 65 x 3 = 195, which is under 200. Check: 196.5 pounds.
3. 6.4 x 5 = 32 miles per week. 32 x 36 = **1,152 miles**. The decimal point is in the tenths place in 6.4, and the answer has no decimal places because 32 x 36 is a whole number, so 6.4 x 5 x 36 = 1152.0 = 1,152.
4. 10.00 / 0.75 = 13.33..., so **13 bags**. 13 x 0.75 = 9.75, change = $0.25. Dividing by 0.75 finds how many 75-cent bags fit into $10.00.
5. 12.6 / 0.7 = 126 / 7 = **18 bags**. Multiplying both numbers by 10 gives 126 / 7, a whole-number division.
6. 2.40 x y = 18.00, so y = 18.00 / 2.40 = 7.5 yards. Lena can buy **7.5 yards** (7.5 is exact, so no rounding is needed since partial yards are sold).
7. 84.6 x 2 = 169.2 miles. 169.2 / 18.0 = **9.4 gallons**. Check: 18 x 9.4 = 169.2. Reasonable because 9.4 mpg-based gallons is close to 170/18 = about 9.4.
8. Answers will vary. Check that the product has the correct number of decimal places: total decimal places in both factors. Example: 2.35 x 4.5 = 10.575.
