## Answer Key

**Part A: Multiple Choice (3 points each)**

1. **B.** *Tinker* holds that students keep their rights at school, and speech may be restricted only with substantial disruption or invasion of others' rights. Explanation: A reflects an unlimited power the Court rejected; C ignores the stated limits; D is not a rule from the case.

2. **A.** *Fraser* allowed discipline for lewd, offensive speech in school, recognizing schools' role in teaching civil discourse. Explanation: B misapplies *Tinker*, which the Court distinguished; C and D invent conditions not in the holding.

3. **C.** *Hazelwood* allows restriction of school-sponsored speech if reasonably related to legitimate pedagogical concerns. Explanation: A is the *Schenck*/*Tinker*-era test for other speech; B is unrelated; D overstates the standard.

4. **A.** *Morse* allowed restriction of student speech at school-sanctioned events that can reasonably be viewed as promoting illegal drug use. Explanation: B and D state rules the Court did not adopt; C is a distractor, since the Court rejected the religious-message argument.

5. **B.** *Mahanoy* found schools have a weaker interest in off-campus speech, so the discipline violated the First Amendment. Explanation: A and C state extremes the Court rejected; D is a fabricated rationale.
