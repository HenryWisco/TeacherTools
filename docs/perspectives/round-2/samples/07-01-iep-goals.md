# IEP Goal Ideas: Jayden, Grade 5

**Name:** ______________________  **Date:** ____________

These are draft ideas for the IEP team to adapt, since goals must be individualized by the team based on the student's needs, data, and input from the family.

## Present Level Summary

- **Functional math:** Counts mixed coins up to 50 cents with 40% accuracy. Identifies penny, nickel, dime, and quarter 4 out of 4. Uses a calculator for single-digit addition.
- **Behavior:** Leaves his seat without permission about 8 times per 30-minute lesson. Needs 3 or more verbal prompts to return to seat.

## Annual Goal Option A: Money Counting

**Condition:** Given a set of mixed coins (pennies, nickels, dimes, quarters) totaling 50 cents or less and a work mat, during a functional math activity.

**Observable behavior:** The student counts the coins and states the total amount in cents.

**Criterion:** With 80% accuracy across 4 out of 5 consecutive data sessions.

**How it will be measured:** Teacher data sheet with 5 trials per session, scored correct or incorrect, recorded weekly.

**Short-term objectives:**
1. Given coins totaling 25 cents or less, the student counts the total with 80% accuracy across 3 consecutive sessions.
2. Given coins totaling up to 50 cents, the student counts the total with 70% accuracy across 3 consecutive sessions.
3. Given a printed coin-value chart, the student counts the total with 80% accuracy, then without the chart with 70% accuracy.

## Annual Goal Option B: Calculator Use for Single-Digit Addition

**Condition:** Given single-digit addition problems (sums up to 18) and a calculator, during independent math practice.

**Observable behavior:** The student enters each problem into the calculator, reads the displayed answer, and records it on the worksheet.

**Criterion:** With 90% accuracy across 4 out of 5 consecutive data sessions.

**How it will be measured:** Teacher-scored worksheets, 10 problems per session, recorded twice weekly.

**Short-term objectives:**
1. Given 10 single-digit addition problems with a calculator, the student enters and records correct answers with 80% accuracy across 3 consecutive sessions.
2. Given 10 single-digit addition problems with a calculator, the student independently checks each answer by re-entering the problem, with 90% accuracy across 3 consecutive sessions.
3. Given 10 single-digit addition problems with a calculator and a visual cue card, the student completes the set independently with 90% accuracy.

## Annual Goal Option C: Remaining Seated During Instruction

**Condition:** During a 30-minute structured lesson in the self-contained classroom, with a visual schedule, a seat-related reinforcement system, and a designated break card.

**Observable behavior:** The student remains in his assigned seat or uses the break card or a requested movement break as agreed.

**Criterion:** Leaves his seat without permission no more than 2 times per 30-minute lesson, across 4 out of 5 consecutive lessons.

**How it will be measured:** Frequency count of unauthorized seat departures per lesson, recorded by the teacher or paraprofessional. Count of verbal prompts needed to return to seat recorded alongside.

**Short-term objectives:**
1. The student remains seated for 10 consecutive minutes of instruction, across 3 consecutive lessons, with no more than 4 unauthorized departures per lesson.
2. When prompted once, the student returns to his seat within 1 minute on 80% of opportunities, across 3 consecutive lessons.
3. The student uses a break card to request a movement break appropriately on at least 3 occasions per lesson, when needed, across 3 consecutive lessons.

## Suggested Accommodations

- Provide a visual schedule showing lesson segments and built-in movement breaks.
- Offer a designated standing or movement option (for example, a standing desk or a classroom job that involves walking).
- Use a break card or signal system so the student can request movement without leaving the seat unannounced.
- Place the student near the instructional area and away from high-traffic or distracting spaces.
- Break math tasks into short segments with clear start and stop points.
- Provide manipulatives (real or play coins) at the desk so hands and attention stay on the task.
- Use a single, calm, consistent prompt, and pair it with a visual cue to reduce the number of verbal prompts needed.
- Provide a calculator and coin-value reference card for math tasks.

## Progress-Monitoring Ideas

- **Coin counting:** Weekly probe of 5 coin sets, scored for accuracy. Graph results against the 80% criterion.
- **Calculator addition:** Twice-weekly worksheet of 10 problems, scored for accuracy and completion time if desired.
- **Seat behavior:** Daily frequency count of unauthorized seat departures and number of prompts needed to return, recorded for each lesson. Review trends every two weeks.
- **Team check-ins:** Monthly review of data with the family and the IEP team to adjust supports and objectives as needed.
