# How Sea Otters Keep Kelp Forests Healthy

**Name:** ______________________  **Date:** ____________

## Reading Passage

Deep under the ocean waves, tall plants called kelp grow in thick groups. Scientists call these groups kelp forests. A kelp forest is home to fish, crabs, snails, sea stars, and many other animals. Kelp can grow very fast, sometimes more than 2 feet in a single day. But even fast-growing kelp needs help to stay healthy.

That help comes from the sea otter. Sea otters are small, furry mammals that spend most of their lives in the water. They eat sea urchins, spiny animals that look like balls covered in sharp needles. Sea urchins love to munch on kelp. When there are too many sea urchins, they can chew through a kelp forest until only bare rock is left. Scientists call this an "urchin barren."

Sea otters keep the sea urchin population under control. An adult sea otter can eat about 25 percent of its body weight in food every day. That means a sea otter weighing 60 pounds may eat around 15 pounds of food each day. Many of those meals are sea urchins. By eating so many urchins, sea otters leave more kelp for the forest to grow.

Scientists learned how important sea otters are by studying places where otters had disappeared. In those areas, sea urchins multiplied and destroyed the kelp. When otters returned to these areas, the sea urchin numbers dropped and the kelp began to grow back. This shows that a single animal can have a big effect on an entire ecosystem.

Sea otters also have a clever habit. They often wrap themselves in kelp so they do not drift away while they sleep. Sometimes you can see groups of otters floating together, tied to the kelp like boats to a dock. This is one more way that otters and kelp depend on each other.
