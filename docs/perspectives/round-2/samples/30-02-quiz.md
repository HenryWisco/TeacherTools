## Answer Key

**1.** B (2 points). The light-dependent reactions capture light energy and produce ATP and NADPH. Option A describes the Calvin cycle, and C and D are incorrect or reversed.

**2.** C (2 points). Photosynthesis occurs in chloroplasts, which contain chlorophyll.

**3.** C (2 points). Aerobic respiration releases carbon dioxide (along with water and energy). Oxygen is a reactant, not a product.

**4.** B (2 points). Plants respire continuously, day and night. Photosynthesis needs light, so it only happens while light is available.

**5.** C (2 points). RuBisCO is the enzyme that fixes carbon dioxide in the Calvin cycle.

**6.** (2 points). 6CO2 + 6H2O + light energy → C6H12O6 + 6O2. Award 1 point for correct reactants and products and 1 point for balancing (6 carbon, 12 hydrogen, 18 oxygen on each side). Words are acceptable if all reactants and products are named.

**7.** (2 points). Water is split during the light-dependent reactions, and the oxygen atoms from the split water are released as O2. Carbon dioxide is used later, in the Calvin cycle, to build sugar, so it does not supply the released oxygen. Award 1 point for stating that water is split and 1 point for the link to released oxygen.

**8.** (2 points). The plant uses carbon dioxide and produces oxygen during photosynthesis. The mouse uses oxygen and produces carbon dioxide during respiration. Award 1 point for each correctly paired gas-use and gas-production statement, for two valid gases total, or 2 points for a complete explanation that names one gas each organism uses and one each produces.

**9.** (2 points). Photosynthesis: inputs are carbon dioxide, water and light energy; outputs are glucose and oxygen; energy change is light energy stored as chemical energy in glucose (endergonic). Aerobic respiration: inputs are glucose and oxygen; outputs are carbon dioxide, water and ATP; energy change is chemical energy released from glucose (exergonic). Award 1 point per row for correct inputs and outputs and energy change.

**10.** (2 points). Anaerobic respiration produces ATP without oxygen, but far less ATP per glucose than aerobic respiration (about 2 ATP compared with about 30 to 32). Lactic acid also builds up, and without oxygen the electron transport chain cannot continue, so the cells cannot sustain high ATP output for long. Award 1 point for explaining that less ATP is produced and 1 point for explaining the role of oxygen and why the process cannot be sustained.
