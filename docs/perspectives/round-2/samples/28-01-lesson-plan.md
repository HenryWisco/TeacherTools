# Lesson Plan: How Steinbeck Presents Loneliness Through Crooks in *Of Mice and Men*, Chapter 4

**Name:** ______________________  **Date:** ____________

## Lesson Overview

- **Grade:** 10
- **Subject:** English Language Arts
- **Class length:** 50 minutes
- **Class size:** 28 students, mixed ability, including 4 English learners
- **Standard:** CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.RL.9-10.3 Analyze how particular elements of a story or drama interact (for example, how setting shapes the characters or plot).
- **Text:** John Steinbeck, *Of Mice and Men*, Chapter 4 (read through the end of the chapter)

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, I can:

1. Explain how Steinbeck uses Crooks's room and possessions to show his isolation.
2. Analyze how Crooks's conversation with Lennie and Candy reveals his loneliness.
3. Explain how a specific setting and character detail work together to develop the theme of loneliness.

## Materials

- Projector and the slides or board version of the three quotations below
- Copies of Chapter 4 or the passages (students may use their own books)
- Student handout (this sheet), one per student
- Index cards or scrap paper for the exit ticket
- Board and markers

## Key Vocabulary

| Word | Meaning in this lesson |
|---|---|
| isolation | The state of being separated from others |
| segregated | Kept apart from a group because of who a person is |
| setting | The time and place where a story happens, and the mood it creates |
| symbol | An object that stands for a larger idea |
| bitterness | Strong resentment built up over time |
| resignation | Acceptance of an unpleasant situation without fighting it |

Preteach the vocabulary at the start of the lesson. For English learners, give a picture or gesture for "isolation" and "segregated," and write the words on the board with a simple definition beside them.

## Close Reading Quotations

Display these on the projector. Students will refer to them throughout the lesson.

**Quote A:** "A rack of harness hung on the wall. Crooks's bed was a long box filled with straw, and over it hung aray of corn-cob-filled gunny sacks to form a wall and a partitioned privacy for the bed."

**Quote B:** "A little rocking chair with a ragged cotton cushion stood by the bed."

**Quote C:** "A man's got to have somebody to be with him... A guy goes nuts if he ain't got nobody. Don't make no difference who the guy is, long's he's with you."

Note for the teacher: Quote C is Crooks speaking, and "Don't make no difference who the guy is" is the line to focus on. Students should notice Crooks's grammar and dialect as part of his voice. Confirm the exact wording against your edition before class, since printings differ slightly.

## Lesson Sequence (50 minutes)

### 1. Hook (5 minutes)

Write on the board: **"What does a room say about the person who lives in it?"**

Ask students to think for 30 seconds, then share with a partner. Take two or three answers. Then say: "Today we will look at Crooks's room in Chapter 4 and ask what it tells us about him."

### 2. Direct Instruction: Vocabulary and Setting (8 minutes)

Review the vocabulary table. Then model the idea that setting can reveal character:

- Explain that Steinbeck describes Crooks's room in detail, which is unusual because the ranch bunkhouse is described as crowded and noisy.
- Point out that Crooks's room is separate, at the edge of the barn. Ask: "Why would a room be placed apart from everyone else?"
- Show Quote A on the projector. Read it aloud. Model one observation: "Crooks has his own space, but it is a corner of the barn, partitioned off with sacks. That suggests he has separation, but also that he has been pushed to the margins."

### 3. Guided Practice: Close Reading (12 minutes)

Display Quote B and Quote A together. Ask the following questions, pausing for responses after each. Use a think-pair-share for the first question.

1. What objects does Steinbeck choose to describe in Crooks's room? (Expected: harness, bed of straw, sacks, rocking chair, cushion.)
2. What do the rocking chair and ragged cushion suggest about Crooks? (Expected: a comfort he has made for himself, but one that is worn and old. It suggests he spends time alone and has little that is new or cared for.)
3. Why might Steinbeck include a chair that rocks, a small object meant for rest and company? (Expected: it suggests a need for comfort and perhaps companionship, even when there is no one to share the space.)

Then display Quote C. Read it aloud, or have a strong reader do so. Ask:

4. What does Crooks admit he needs? (Expected: someone to be with him.)
5. Why does he say "Don't make no difference who the guy is"? (Expected: he is willing to accept any company because he has been without it for so long. This shows how deep his loneliness is.)

For English learners, put a sentence frame on the board: "Crooks needs ____ because ____." Allow them to answer with one word or a drawing.

### 4. Independent Practice (15 minutes)

Students complete the handout below individually. Circulate and check in, prioritizing the English learners and students who need support.

**Analysis Task:** Choose one of the three quotations. Write a paragraph of 5 to 7 sentences that answers this question: *How does this detail show Crooks's loneliness?* Include the quotation, explain what it shows, and connect it to the idea of isolation.

Paragraph frame (optional, for support):
"In Chapter 4, Steinbeck writes, '[quote].' This detail shows that Crooks is lonely because ____. The reader can see this through ____. This suggests that Crooks ____."

### 5. Closure and Exit Ticket (5 minutes)

Ask one or two students to share a sentence from their paragraph. Then have each student answer the exit ticket on an index card:

**Exit ticket:** In one or two sentences, explain how Steinbeck uses Crooks's room to show loneliness.

Collect the cards as students leave.

## Differentiation

**For students who need support:**
- Provide the paragraph frame in the independent task.
- Allow students to choose the quotation they find easiest to start with.
- Read Quote C aloud twice and pause to check comprehension.
- Give a word bank: isolated, alone, separated, comfort, longing, pushed aside.

**For English learners:**
- Preteach vocabulary with visuals and gestures.
- Use sentence frames for oral and written answers.
- Accept drawings or single words during guided practice before asking for full sentences.
- Pair with a supportive partner for think-pair-share.

**For students ready for more:**
- Ask: Steinbeck gives Crooks a rocking chair and a cushion, but also describes his room as partitioned off. How do these two details work together?
- Ask: Why might the setting of the barn, rather than the bunkhouse, be important for the theme of loneliness?
- Extension paragraph: Compare Crooks's loneliness to Curley's wife's, using one quotation from each. Consider how each character's loneliness is shaped by the ranch.

## Formative Assessment and Expected Answers

Use the exit ticket to check understanding.

| Level | Expected response |
|---|---|
| Proficient | Names a specific detail from Crooks's room (such as the partition, the chair, or the straw bed) and explains that it shows separation or a need for companionship. |
| Developing | Names a detail but explains it only generally, such as "Crooks is lonely because he lives in a barn." |
| Beginning | Identifies a detail without connecting it to loneliness. |

Look for patterns in the exit tickets. If many students are at the Developing or Beginning level, reteach the link between setting and character at the start of the next class.

## Teacher Reflection (for mentor teacher observation)

- Note how students responded during the think-pair-share and whether all four English learners participated.
- Record which students needed the paragraph frame and whether they were able to move beyond it.
- Reflect on pacing: if guided practice runs long, shorten the independent task to four sentences and leave the extension for homework.
