# Exit Ticket: Comparing Fractions with the Same Numerator or Denominator

**Name:** ______________________  **Date:** ____________

Directions: Read each question. Use your fraction strips or draw a number line to help you. Write >, =, or < in the box. Then finish the sentence to explain your thinking. You may use the sentence frame to help you.

**Sentence frame:** I know that ______________________ is ______________________ than ______________________ because ______________________.

Example: I know that 3/8 is less than 5/8 because both have 8 equal parts, and 3 parts is fewer than 5 parts.

## Answer Key

1. 1/3 < 2/3. Both fractions have the same denominator (3 equal parts), so the fraction with more parts shaded is greater. Correct reasoning: "Both have 3 equal parts, and 1 part is less than 2 parts."
   - If a student writes >, they may be comparing only the numbers 1 and 2 without considering the denominator, or may be thinking of the whole number ordering without understanding that the parts are equal in size. Reteach: lay 1/3 and 2/3 strips end to end on a sheet of paper and have the student point to where each ends on a number line from 0 to 1.

2. 3/4 > 1/4. Both have the same denominator (4 equal parts), so 3 parts are more than 1 part. Correct reasoning: "Both have 4 equal parts, and 3 parts is more than 1 part."
   - If a student writes <, they may be reading the fractions as if the smaller bottom number makes the fraction larger, or may be reversing the comparison. Reteach: count the shaded parts aloud on a fourths strip, one part at a time, then compare the two counts.

3. 5/6 > 5/8. Both have the same numerator (5 parts), but sixths are bigger pieces than eighths, so 5 sixths is more than 5 eighths. Correct reasoning: "Both have 5 parts, but sixths are larger pieces than eighths, so 5/6 is greater."
   - If a student writes <, they may be thinking that a larger denominator means a larger fraction. Reteach: place a sixths strip directly above an eighths strip, both 5 parts shaded, and ask which shaded part is bigger.

4. 2/8 = 2/8. The fractions are identical, so they are equal. Correct reasoning: "They have the same number of parts and the same size parts."
   - If a student writes > or <, they may be comparing the strips without lining them up, or may think identical fractions must differ. Reteach: place two identical 2/8 strips on top of each other and show they match exactly.

5. Self-rating: Accept any choice. If a student circles "Not yet" or "Getting there," plan a small-group reteach using fraction strips with the same numerator or denominator before the next lesson.
