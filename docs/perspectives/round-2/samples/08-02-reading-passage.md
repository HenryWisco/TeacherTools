## Part A: Reading Passage (English)

### Water on the Move

Water is always moving. It goes up into the sky, falls back down, and then moves again. This never-ending journey is called the water cycle.

The water cycle begins with the sun. The sun heats water in oceans, lakes, and rivers. When water gets warm enough, it changes into a gas called water vapor. This change is called evaporation. You cannot see water vapor, but it is in the air all around you.

Water vapor rises high into the sky. There, the air is cooler. The vapor cools down and changes back into tiny drops of liquid water. These drops join together to form clouds. This change is called condensation.

When the drops in a cloud get big and heavy, they fall to the ground. This is called precipitation. Precipitation can be rain, snow, sleet, or hail. In cold places, water may fall as snow. In warm places, it usually falls as rain.

After precipitation, the water goes in different directions. Some soaks into the ground. This water is called groundwater, and it can be stored underground for a long time. Some water flows over the ground into rivers, lakes, and oceans. Plants also take in water through their roots. Later, the plants release water vapor into the air through their leaves. This is called transpiration.

Then the cycle starts again. The sun heats the water, and the water rises into the sky. The water cycle has no beginning and no end. The same water has been moving around Earth for millions of years.

### El agua en movimiento

El agua siempre está en movimiento. Sube al cielo, vuelve a caer y luego vuelve a moverse. Este viaje que nunca termina se llama el ciclo del agua.

El ciclo del agua comienza con el sol. El sol calienta el agua de los océanos, lagos y ríos. Cuando el agua se calienta lo suficiente, se convierte en un gas llamado vapor de agua. Este cambio se llama evaporación. No puedes ver el vapor de agua, pero está en el aire a tu alrededor.

El vapor de agua sube muy alto en el cielo. Allí el aire está más frío. El vapor se enfría y vuelve a convertirse en gotas diminutas de agua líquida. Estas gotas se juntan y forman las nubes. Este cambio se llama condensación.

Cuando las gotas de una nube se hacen grandes y pesadas, caen al suelo. Esto se llama precipitación. La precipitación puede ser lluvia, nieve, aguanieve o granizo. En lugares fríos, el agua puede caer como nieve. En lugares cálidos, normalmente cae como lluvia.

Después de la precipitación, el agua se mueve en distintas direcciones. Una parte se filtra en la tierra. Esta agua se llama agua subterránea, y puede quedar guardada bajo tierra durante mucho tiempo. Otra parte corre por la superficie hacia los ríos, lagos y océanos. Las plantas también absorben agua por sus raíces. Más tarde, las plantas liberan vapor de agua al aire por sus hojas. Esto se llama transpiración.

Entonces el ciclo vuelve a empezar. El sol calienta el agua, y el agua sube al cielo. El ciclo del agua no tiene principio ni fin. El mismo agua se ha estado moviendo alrededor de la Tierra durante millones de años.

## Level 2 Reading: The Water Cycle (English)

The water cycle is the constant movement of water between the ground, the air, and living things. The sun powers the cycle by heating water in oceans, lakes, and rivers. Heated water changes into water vapor through evaporation. Water vapor is invisible, but it is always present in the air.

As water vapor rises, it reaches cooler air and condenses into tiny droplets that form clouds. When droplets combine and become heavy, they fall as precipitation, such as rain, snow, sleet, or hail.

After precipitation, water follows several paths. Some soaks into the soil and becomes groundwater, which can stay underground for years. Some runs over the surface into streams and eventually the ocean. Plants absorb water through their roots and release water vapor through their leaves in a process called transpiration. The water then returns to the air, and the cycle continues.

## Level 3 Reading: The Water Cycle (English)

The water cycle is often described as a loop, but it is better understood as a system of connected pathways. Energy from the sun drives evaporation from oceans, lakes, and rivers, which together supply most of the water vapor in the atmosphere. Because the air higher in the sky is cooler, vapor condenses onto tiny particles, forming droplets that become clouds. When droplets grow large enough, they fall as precipitation, and the form it takes depends on the temperature of the air the droplets pass through.

Once water reaches the ground, its path depends on the land. On hard, flat surfaces, water runs off quickly into streams. On soil, more water soaks in and becomes groundwater, which can move slowly underground and stay there for a very long time. Plants add another route: through transpiration, they return a large share of the water they absorb to the air. Some ecosystems, such as forests, recycle much of their own rainfall this way.

Because water is continuously reused, the water you drink may once have been part of a glacier, an ocean, or a cloud over another continent. The cycle does not produce new water. It moves the same water through different places and forms.

- **condense**: to change from gas to liquid
- **runoff**: water that flows over land instead of soaking in
- **ecosystem**: a community of living things and their environment
- **glacier**: a large mass of slow-moving ice
