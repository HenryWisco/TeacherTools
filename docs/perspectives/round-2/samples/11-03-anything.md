# Fractions Lesson: Adding, Subtracting, and Dividing Fractions

**Grade level:** Combined 5th and 6th grade
**Length:** 45 minutes
**Standards:** 5.NF.A.1 (add and subtract fractions with unlike denominators); 6.NS.A.1 (divide fractions by fractions)

**Name:** ______________________  **Date:** ____________

## Shared Warm-Up (5 minutes)

Teacher directions: Write these on the board before students arrive. Students answer silently, then compare with a partner.

1. Which is larger, 1/2 or 3/8? Explain how you know.
2. A rancher used 1/4 bale of hay in the morning and 1/3 bale in the afternoon. About how much hay is that in total?
3. How many 1/2-cup scoops of feed can you get from 3 cups? Explain your answer.

## Part A: Adding and Subtracting Fractions (5th Grade) (about 15 minutes)

Directions: Find a common denominator before you add or subtract. Simplify your answer if you can.

Example: A family hauls water in two trips. The first trip fills 1/2 of a tank and the second trip fills 1/3 of a tank. How much of the tank is full?
1/2 = 3/6 and 1/3 = 2/6, so 3/6 + 2/6 = 5/6 of the tank.

## Part B: Dividing Fractions by Fractions (6th Grade) (about 15 minutes)

Directions: To divide by a fraction, multiply by its reciprocal (flip the second fraction and multiply). Simplify your answer.

Example: Stew needs 3/4 cup of broth for each bowl. A pot has 3/2 cups of broth. How many bowls can be made?
(3/2) divided by (3/4) = (3/2) x (4/3) = 12/6 = 2 bowls.

## Answer Key

Warm-Up:
1. 1/2 is larger. 1/2 = 4/8, and 4/8 is larger than 3/8.
2. About 1/2 bale (1/4 + 1/3 = 7/12, a little more than 1/2).
3. 6 scoops (3 divided by 1/2 = 6).

Part A:
1. 3/8 + 1/4 = 3/8 + 2/8 = 5/8 of a bag.
2. 2/3 + 1/6 = 4/6 + 1/6 = 5/6 foot.
3. 3/4 + 1/8 = 6/8 + 1/8 = 7/8 cup.
4. 5/6 - 1/2 = 5/6 - 3/6 = 2/6 = 1/3 bale more.
5. 7/8 - 2/8 = 5/8 of the tank used. (1/4 = 2/8 remains, so 7/8 - 2/8 = 5/8 used.)

Hmm, correction for item 5: the amount used is 7/8 - 1/4 = 7/8 - 2/8 = 5/8 of a full load.

Part B:
6. (3/4) divided by (1/8) = (3/4) x 8 = 24/4 = 6 sections.
7. (4/3) divided by (2/3) = (4/3) x (3/2) = 12/6 = 2 batches.
8. (5/2) divided by (5/6) = (5/2) x (6/5) = 30/10 = 3 trips.
9. (3/4) divided by (1/12) = (3/4) x 12 = 36/4 = 9 portions.
10. 2 divided by (1/3) = 2 x 3 = 6 servings.
