# Why Sleep Matters for Growing Bodies and Brains

**Name:** ______________________  **Date:** ____________

## Part A: English

### Reading Passage

Your body is always busy, even when you are asleep. While you rest, your body works hard to grow, heal, and get ready for the next day. Sleep is one of the most important things a growing body needs.

Most children your age need about 9 to 12 hours of sleep each night. That is more time than many adults need. When children get enough sleep, they often feel calmer, pay better attention in class, and have more energy to play and move.

Sleep helps your brain, too. During sleep, your brain sorts through what you learned during the day. It stores new facts and skills in places where you can find them later. This is one reason a good night's sleep can make it easier to remember spelling words or the steps of a new game.

Sleep also helps your body. Your muscles repair themselves while you rest. Your bones grow stronger, and your body fights off germs more easily. When you are tired, you may get sick more often, and it can be harder to run, jump, and keep going during recess or gym class.

Many things can make it hard to get enough sleep. Some children share a bed or a room with someone who moves a lot or wakes up often. Some families have schedules that make bedtimes change from night to night. Some children feel worried or are not sure how to calm down. None of these things is anyone's fault. Families and children can work together to find small changes that help, such as a regular bedtime routine, a quiet place to wind down, and screens put away before bed.

Sleep is not extra. It is part of how your body and brain grow strong, so it deserves a regular place in your day.

El sueño no es algo extra. Es parte de cómo tu cuerpo y tu cerebro se fortalecen al crecer, así que merece un lugar fijo en tu día.
