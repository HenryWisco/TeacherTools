# Writing Prompts: Loneliness and Belonging in *Of Mice and Men*

**Name:** ______________________  **Date:** ____________

Directions: Choose and complete the prompts your teacher assigns. Each prompt is labeled with its kind of writing (narrative, argumentative, expository, descriptive, or reflective). Use the scaffolding line to get started. Write in complete paragraphs unless the prompt says otherwise. Write about the novel and its characters, not about your own personal experiences.

1. **(Argumentative)** George tells Lennie, "Guys like us, that work on ranches, are the loneliest guys in the world." Do you agree that the ranch workers in the novel are the loneliest people in the world? Take a position and support it with at least two specific examples from the novel.
   *Scaffolding: "I agree / disagree that ___ because ___. For example, in Chapter ___, ___ shows that ___."*

2. **(Expository)** Explain how the bunkhouse setting shapes the characters' relationships with one another. Consider where they sleep, what they own, and how they spend their free time.
   *Scaffolding: What do the men do in the bunkhouse when they are not working? Why might the bunkhouse keep them apart as much as it brings them together?*

3. **(Narrative)** Write a short scene, about one page long, that takes place in the bunkhouse on the night after Lennie and George arrive. Show one character who is lonely and one who is looking for someone to talk to. Use dialogue and at least one detail about setting.
   *Scaffolding: Start with a specific detail, such as the sound of a card game or the smell of the room. Who speaks first, and what do they want?*

4. **(Descriptive)** Choose one of the minor characters: Candy, Crooks, Curley's wife, or Carlson. Describe that character's living space or possessions in the novel, and explain what those objects reveal about his or her loneliness.
   *Scaffolding: What objects does this character keep? What do they say about the character's hopes or fears?*

5. **(Reflective)** Think about the moment when Candy reveals that he would like to join George and Lennie's dream of owning land. Write about why this dream matters so much to Candy. What does it promise him that his current life does not?
   *Scaffolding: "Candy's dream offers him ___. I think this matters because ___."*

6. **(Argumentative)** Some readers believe Curley's wife is a villain. Others believe she is a lonely person who has been trapped by circumstances. Which view do you find more convincing? Support your argument with evidence from her dialogue and from how other characters treat her.
   *Scaffolding: Identify one quotation that supports each view. Which one carries more weight, and why?*

7. **(Expository)** Explain the role of the dog in the novel and why Carlson's treatment of it is significant to the theme of loneliness and belonging. Be sure to connect the dog's fate to the characters' fates.
   *Scaffolding: What happens to the dog? How does Candy react, and what does his reaction suggest about his own situation?*

8. **(Narrative)** Write a letter from Lennie to George, written after Lennie has been alone for some time. Use Lennie's voice and vocabulary. Include at least one memory of the friends' dream and one question Lennie wants to ask.
   *Scaffolding: What would Lennie remember most? How would he describe his feelings in simple, honest language?*

9. **(Descriptive)** Describe the final scene by the Salinas River. Use sensory details (sight, sound, smell, touch) to create the setting and the mood, and show how the landscape reflects the characters' emotions.
   *Scaffolding: What do you notice first about the river and the hills? How does the setting change from the beginning of the scene to the end?*

10. **(Reflective)** The novel suggests that the dream of belonging is powerful but may be impossible to achieve for men in this time and place. What do you think is the most important lesson the novel offers about friendship? Write about how the novel has changed or deepened your understanding of this idea.
    *Scaffolding: "The novel teaches that friendship ___. Before reading, I thought ___, but now I think ___."*

## Writing Checklist

Use this checklist before you submit each piece of writing.

- [ ] I answered the prompt directly and stayed on topic.
- [ ] My claim, main idea, or narrative focus is clear in my opening.
- [ ] I used at least two specific details or quotations from the novel, and I cited chapter or page numbers.
- [ ] I explained how each piece of evidence supports my point.
- [ ] My paragraphs each focus on one main idea.
- [ ] I used the writing type's features (for example, dialogue and setting for narrative, or a clear thesis for argument).
- [ ] I checked spelling, punctuation, and capitalization, including character names.
- [ ] I reread my work aloud to catch sentences that need revision.

## Answer Key

These prompts are open-ended and have no single correct answer. Assess student writing with the rubric your teacher provides, looking for a clear focus, textual evidence, logical reasoning, and control of conventions. Accept any well-supported position or interpretation.
