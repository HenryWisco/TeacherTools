# Rubric: Timed Literary Argument Essay on *Beloved*

**Name:** ______________________  **Date:** ____________

Assignment: In a timed essay, argue a claim about how the novel's fractured chronology shapes its treatment of memory. Support your argument with close reading of at least two passages from *Beloved*.

Total: 20 points (four criteria, each scored on a 5-point scale, with 5 levels). Scores are given in whole numbers across the levels shown. Each criterion's levels are scored as follows:

- Level 5 (Exemplary): 5 points
- Level 4 (Proficient): 4 points
- Level 3 (Developing): 3 points
- Level 2 (Beginning): 2 points
- Level 1 (Inadequate): 1 point

Each criterion is worth 5 points, for a total of 20 points.

## Rubric Table

| Criterion | Level 5 (5 pts) | Level 4 (4 pts) | Level 3 (3 pts) | Level 2 (2 pts) | Level 1 (1 pt) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Thesis** | States a arguable, specific claim about how the non-linear structure shapes the presentation of memory in *Beloved* (for example, that Sethe's repeated returns to 124 and the withheld backstory make memory a site of trauma that the narrative must reconstruct rather than recall). The thesis previews the reasoning the essay will follow and is placed early. | States an arguable claim about the fractured chronology and memory that is clear and specific. The thesis previews the main reasoning but may be slightly general (for example, "the structure shows how memory is painful"). | States a claim that connects the novel's structure to memory, but the claim is broad or partly descriptive (for example, "Morrison uses flashbacks to show Sethe's past"). It does not clearly preview the argument. | States a topic rather than a claim (for example, "This essay is about memory in *Beloved*"), or the claim does not address chronology. | No identifiable thesis, or the claim is unrelated to the prompt. |
| **Evidence and Commentary** | Uses at least two well-chosen passages from *Beloved* (for example, the opening of Chapter 1, Sethe's memory of the schoolteacher's nephews, or the "Rememory" passage in which the past persists in the house). Each quotation is introduced, accurately cited, and followed by commentary that explains how the specific language, sequence, or shift in time produces the effect being discussed. Commentary consistently links evidence back to the thesis. | Uses at least two relevant passages with accurate citations. Commentary explains most quotations and connects them to the chronology and memory, though one piece of evidence is explained more briefly or is less precisely tied to the thesis. | Uses relevant evidence, but some quotations are summarized rather than analyzed, or commentary restates the passage instead of explaining how it works (for example, "Sethe remembers the milk and this shows she is sad"). Citations may be inconsistent. | Evidence is limited, mostly plot summary, or only one passage is used. Commentary is minimal or does not connect to the thesis. Citations are missing or inaccurate. | No textual evidence, or evidence is irrelevant or invented. |
| **Sophistication** | Shows an original, nuanced understanding of how Morrison's fractured chronology complicates the act of remembering. The essay considers a counterargument or tension (for example, that the novel's refusal to narrate Sethe's infanticide in a single chronological sequence is both protective and accusatory) and explores implications, such as what the structure asks of the reader or how the past returns as "rememory" to dismantle the boundary between past and present. Insights go beyond the obvious. | Shows a clear and thoughtful understanding of the relationship between structure and memory. The essay offers some complication or implication, though the reasoning may stay within expected readings of the novel. | Shows a basic understanding. The essay identifies that the novel is nonlinear and that memory matters, but the analysis mostly repeats commonplace observations without developing them. | Understanding is superficial or mostly plot-based. Little or no analysis of how the structure works. | Shows no understanding of the novel's structure or its relation to memory. |
| **Control of Prose** | Writing is precise, varied, and concise, with a clear sense of audience and purpose. Sentences are controlled, and transitions show how each point builds on the last. Literary terms (such as analepsis, narrative voice, or "rememory") are used accurately. Errors are rare and do not affect meaning. | Writing is clear and organized, with appropriate word choice and generally controlled sentences. Transitions are present and mostly effective. Literary terms are used accurately. Minor errors do not interfere with meaning. | Writing is understandable but uneven. Sentences may be repetitive or awkward, and transitions are sometimes mechanical. Some errors may distract the reader. Literary terms are sometimes misused. | Writing is frequently unclear. Errors often interfere with meaning, the organization is hard to follow, and vocabulary is limited or inaccurate. | Writing is largely incoherent, or the response is too brief to assess. |

## Student Checklist

Before you finish, check that:

- [ ] My thesis makes a specific, arguable claim about how *Beloved*'s non-linear structure shapes memory.
- [ ] My thesis appears early and previews the reasons I will give.
- [ ] I use at least two passages from *Beloved*, quoted accurately with citations.
- [ ] After every quotation, I explain how the language, sequence, or shift in time creates the effect I describe.
- [ ] Every paragraph connects back to my thesis.
- [ ] I acknowledge at least one complication, counterargument, or implication of my reading.
- [ ] I use literary terms accurately (for example, analepsis, narrative voice, rememory).
- [ ] I have proofread for errors that could confuse a reader.
