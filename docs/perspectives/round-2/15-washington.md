# Dr. Washington, elementary school principal
**Verdict:** Would allow with conditions. I would let teachers use it for drafts, not recommend it officially, until the owner is named and the leftover placeholders are removed.
**In one sentence:** The worksheet was accurate and the family email was nearly ready to send, but the site has no named owner and shows unfinished legal notes. Those are things I would need before telling staff it is safe to use.

## What I did
- Pages visited: home (/), /tools, /library (first page), /library/equivalent-fractions-worksheet-grade-4, /about, /privacy, /terms.
- Forms looked at: parent-email, worksheet.
- Document 1: Family Email, Grade 2 field trip, warm tone, English + Spanish. Sample file: C:\Users\henry\Desktop\TeacherTools\docs\perspectives\round-2\samples\15-01-parent-email.md
- Document 2: Worksheet, Grade 2 math, adding and subtracting within 100 with regrouping, 10 questions, mixed style, with a note to families. Sample file: C:\Users\henry\Desktop\TeacherTools\docs\perspectives\round-2\samples\15-02-worksheet.md
- I did not use the third document. Two documents plus one library resource were enough.

## Problems found
1. **The family email ends with an "Answer Key" section.** Where: end of document 1. I saw "## Answer Key / Not applicable. This document contains no questions or problems for students." It also opens with a "Name: ____ Date: ____" line. Both are worksheet furniture. A parent would see them if the teacher pastes it in without noticing. Seriousness: major. Fix: the Family Email tool should never add a name/date line or an answer-key section.
2. **Unfinished legal notes are visible to the public.** Where: /privacy and /terms. I saw "Site owner: review this page with a qualified adviser before launch, and add your business name and address." Also, "Contact: ... the site owner" names no one. Seriousness: blocker for me as an approver. Fix: remove the notes and name the operator, with a real email address and mailing address.
3. **There is nothing for a school to check against.** Where: /privacy. The page does not mention FERPA, COPPA or a data-processing agreement. It does not say whether Anthropic keeps or trains on what teachers type. It says what is typed "is sent to our AI provider (Anthropic)... not stored by us". It does say students must not use the site and that student names must not be entered. Seriousness: major. Fix: add a short "For schools and districts" section. It should say plainly what is and is not collected, and whether the AI provider retains the text. It should state that no student data is ever required.
4. **The email left the due date as a placeholder even though I gave the date of the trip.** Where: document 1. I saw "Return the signed permission slip by Tuesday, [date]." Many placeholders ([phone or email], [Teacher name], [School name]) are expected. This one is not, because the site could have worked it out or asked. In Spanish, "antes de las 2:15 p. m." says "before 2:15" where the English says "by 2:15". Seriousness: minor. Fix: leave the placeholders but highlight them, or add a "fill these in before sending" checklist at the top.
5. **The "reviewed library" claim is not backed by any detail.** Where: /library and /about, "reviewed before being listed". I could not see who reviews or how. Seriousness: minor to major. A principal fears the one bad worksheet. Fix: say who reviews and what is checked. Show a "last reviewed" date on each resource.
6. **The library worksheet's teacher notes refer to "Problem 12" and "Problem 14", but the problems are not numbered on the page.** Where: /library/equivalent-fractions-worksheet-grade-4. A teacher cannot find them without counting. The math itself was all correct. Seriousness: minor. Fix: number the problems.
7. **"Mixed" question style produced almost no variety.** Where: document 2. All seven computation questions are bare equations, followed by three word problems. That is fine for Grade 2, but not really "mixed". Seriousness: minor, an opinion.

## What worked
- I checked every answer in the Grade 2 worksheet: 35, 52, 82, 84, 23, 36, 36, 64, 23, 83 and 17. All are correct, and both worked examples (47+25=72, 52-17=35) are right. The count matched the 10 I asked for.
- The family note on the worksheet was well pitched for Grade 2 and I would send it home. It had practical tips and kind wording.
- The Spanish email read naturally, in a polite register, with the right accents and with 12-hour time in Spanish style.
- The library's Grade 4 equivalent-fractions worksheet had a correct answer key. It also had a useful note on the common mistake of adding the same number to the top and bottom.
- The About and Terms pages are honest that AI makes mistakes and that teachers must review everything. "Always review materials" shows in every page footer. That is the sentence I would quote to staff.
- It has no accounts, tells users to leave out student names, and says ads never appear on printed documents or on the family, report comment and IEP tools.

## What I could not check
- How it looks on screen or in print, and whether the Print, Word and Google Docs buttons work.
- Whether the saved "My documents" really stay only in the browser.
- Whether the ads are acceptable once real ones are shown (only "Ad space" placeholders appeared).
- Whether the Family Email would handle a sensitive subject (a behavior concern) well. I did not test it.

## What I would say to a colleague about this site
"It is fine for drafting a newsletter or a practice page, and it saved me time. But you must read every line before it goes home, because it once added an answer key to a parent letter. Do not type any student names or details. Don't use the IEP or report card tools for anything official until we know more about who runs the site. I will wait on a formal recommendation until they name the owner and publish a clear privacy statement for schools."
