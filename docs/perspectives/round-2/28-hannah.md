# Hannah, secondary English student teacher

**Verdict:** Would use, with conditions. I would use it for writing prompts and for a lesson skeleton, but not for anything that quotes a real book, and not until I have asked my supervisor whether it is allowed.

**In one sentence:** The site is honest that an AI writes the documents, but it gives me no help with the question I actually have (do I have to tell anyone, and how), and the lesson plan it wrote for me contained invented "quotations" from Of Mice and Men that would have embarrassed me in front of my mentor teacher.

## What I did

Pages visited: home page, Tools list, the Writing Prompts tool page, the Lesson Plan and Writing Prompts forms, the free-form "anything" form, Library, one library resource ("12 Argumentative Writing Prompts with Counterclaims", Grade 10), About, Privacy, Terms, Support us, My documents.

Documents generated (3):

1. `28-01-lesson-plan.md` - Lesson Plan tool. Grade 10 English, 50 minutes, "How Steinbeck presents loneliness through Crooks in Of Mice and Men, Chapter 4", standard CCSS RL.9-10.3. I asked for 3 short direct quotations from Chapter 4, no technology except a projector, 28 students with 4 English learners, mentor teacher observing.
2. `28-02-writing-prompts.md` - Writing Prompts tool. 10 mixed prompts on loneliness and belonging to go with the novel, each labelled with its kind of writing, avoiding prompts that ask for personal trauma.
3. `28-03-anything.md` - the free-form box. I asked for an honest disclosure note for my mentor teacher and university supervisor, plus a checklist of what to verify before teaching.

(Samples are in `C:\Users\henry\Desktop\TeacherTools\docs\perspectives\round-2\samples\`.)

## Problems found

### 1. The lesson plan's "direct quotations" from the novel are not in the novel (blocker for me)
Where: `28-01-lesson-plan.md`, section "Close Reading Quotations", and everything built on it (steps 2 and 3, the extension questions, the exit-ticket expected answers).

What I saw:
- Quote A: "A rack of harness hung on the wall. Crooks's bed was a long box filled with straw, and over it hung aray of corn-cob-filled gunny sacks to form a wall and a partitioned privacy for the bed." Only "a long box filled with straw" matches the book (Steinbeck: "Crooks' bunk was a long box filled with straw, on which his blankets were flung"). The gunny-sack partition is not in the chapter. There is also a typo, "aray".
- Quote B: "A little rocking chair with a ragged cotton cushion stood by the bed." I cannot find a rocking chair anywhere in Crooks's room. Three of the five guided questions, one extension question and the "Proficient" exit-ticket answer ("the partition, the chair") depend on this object.
- Quote C: opens "A man's got to have somebody to be with him..." The real line is "A guy needs somebody - to be near him." The rest of Quote C ("A guy goes nuts if he ain't got nobody...") is correct.

The plan also leaves out the details every teacher uses for this scene: Crooks's books, the tattered dictionary, the California civil code, the gold-rimmed spectacles, the liniment.

The teacher note says "Confirm the exact wording against your edition before class, since printings differ slightly." That is misleading. These are not printing differences; two of the three quotations are made up. A tired student teacher would read that note as "it is basically right".

Fix I would want: when a request asks for direct quotations from a published book and no text was pasted in, do not invent them. Either leave clearly marked blanks ("Insert quotation from p.__ describing Crooks's bunk") or ask me to paste the passage into the "Your own text" box. If quotations are produced anyway, put a plain warning directly above them: "These quotations were written by AI from memory and may not appear in the book. Check every one."

### 2. No guidance anywhere on disclosure, attribution or whether student teachers may use this (major)
Where: /about, /terms, /privacy, the tool pages, and the footer of every page.

What I saw: About says "Documents are written by an AI model". Terms says "Your documents are yours to use" and "Documents are written by AI and may contain errors. You are responsible for reviewing materials". That is all. Nothing about whether or how to tell a school, mentor, university or parents that a document was AI-drafted; nothing for trainees whose institutions restrict AI; no suggested wording for a credit line.

Fix: a short "Using AI-made materials honestly" section on About (or its own page, linked in the footer): check your school or university policy first; a one-line suggested disclosure ("Drafted with an AI tool, checked and edited by [teacher]"); a note that student teachers should ask their supervisor before using it for assessed planning.

### 3. Generated documents carry no mark that they are AI-drafted, and there is no option to add one (major, partly opinion)
Where: all three samples; the buttons listed on the tool page (Print, Word, Copy for Google Docs, Edit, Large print, Share).

What I saw: none of the three documents has a footer or note such as "Drafted with AI - review before use". I saw no checkbox to add one. For me the honest path should be the easy one. Other teachers may not want a footer on student handouts, so it should be a choice.

Fix: an optional tick-box "Add a small 'AI-assisted draft, reviewed by teacher' line", on by default on teacher-facing documents such as lesson plans and off on student handouts.

### 4. The standard I typed in was given the wrong description (major)
Where: `28-01-lesson-plan.md`, "Lesson Overview".

What I saw: "CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.RL.9-10.3 Analyze how particular elements of a story or drama interact (for example, how setting shapes the characters or plot)." That wording is the Grade 7 standard (RL.7.3). RL.9-10.3 is about how complex characters develop, interact with other characters and advance the plot or develop the theme. The objectives were then written to the wrong (setting-based) standard. My supervisor checks standards alignment.

Fix: keep a correct lookup of standard codes to their text, or print only the code I typed and tell me to paste in the wording.

### 5. The lesson timings add up to 45 minutes, not the 50 I chose (minor)
Where: `28-01-lesson-plan.md`, "Lesson Sequence (50 minutes)".

What I saw: 5 + 8 + 12 + 15 + 5 = 45. Also, "Preteach the vocabulary at the start of the lesson" but the vocabulary is scheduled in step 2, after the hook.

Fix: make the steps total the chosen class length and show the running clock (0-5, 5-13 ...).

### 6. The plan avoids the reason Crooks is isolated (major, a judgement call)
Where: `28-01-lesson-plan.md`, steps 2 and 3.

What I saw: the plan asks "Why would a room be placed apart from everyone else?" and lists "segregated" as vocabulary, but never says that Crooks is the only Black man on the ranch and is barred from the bunkhouse because of racism, and gives no advice on the racial slur that appears throughout this chapter. A new teacher needs exactly that advice before reading Chapter 4 aloud with a class.

Fix: for texts with well-known sensitive content, include a short "Before you teach this" note covering the context and how to handle offensive language in the text.

### 7. The lesson plan is confused about whether it is a teacher page or a student page (minor)
Where: `28-01-lesson-plan.md`, top line and "Materials".

What I saw: a "Name: ____ Date: ____" line at the top of a teacher's lesson plan, and Materials lists "Student handout (this sheet), one per student", although the sheet contains expected answers and teacher notes. The disclosure letter (`28-03-anything.md`) also starts with a student-style Name/Date line. In the prompts sheet the "Answer Key" tells the reader to use "the rubric your teacher provides", as if speaking to the student.

Fix: only put the Name/Date line on student-facing pages; put the student task on its own separate page; address the answer key to the teacher.

### 8. One writing prompt has a premise that does not fit the book, and the directions clash with a prompt (minor)
Where: `28-02-writing-prompts.md`, prompt 8 and prompt 10.

What I saw: Prompt 8: "Write a letter from Lennie to George, written after Lennie has been alone for some time." Lennie is never apart from George for any length of time, and writing a letter is hard to square with the character; students will ask when this is supposed to happen. Separately, the directions say "Write about the novel and its characters, not about your own personal experiences", but prompt 10's starter is "Before reading, I thought ___, but now I think ___". I asked only to avoid personal trauma, not all personal reflection.

Fix: reword prompt 8 (for example, "Write what Lennie might say to George while hiding in the brush"); soften the direction to "You do not need to share anything private".

### 9. The library says resources are "reviewed" but not by whom, or whether they were AI-written (minor)
Where: /library ("Ready-made and reviewed"), /about ("reviewed before being listed"), and the resource page I opened.

What I saw: no author, no "AI-drafted, checked by ..." line, no date on the resource page. After finding invented quotations in my own lesson plan, "reviewed" needs to mean something I can see.

Fix: one line on each library resource saying how it was made and what the review covered.

Small extras: the disclosure note left "[Website Name]" as a blank even though I said "this website"; and the Privacy and Terms pages still show "Site owner: review this page with a qualified adviser before launch", with no real contact address for removal requests.

## What worked

- The site does not hide the AI. The page title, About and Terms all say so, and the footer on every page says "Always review materials before using them with your class."
- The writing prompts were good. I asked for 10 and got 10, each labelled with its kind of writing, a sensible mix, with a starter line for each. The George quotation in prompt 1 ("Guys like us, that work on ranches, are the loneliest guys in the world") is correct. It respected my request about personal trauma.
- The disclosure note from the free-form box was better than I expected. It did not tell me it was fine. It said "I would welcome your guidance on whether this use is acceptable" and its checklist includes "Any quotation attributed to a text is compared word for word against the actual text" and "You have confirmed with your university supervisor how AI use should be documented". This is the guidance the site itself should carry.
- The lesson plan followed my instructions on class size, English learners, no technology, and the mentor observation. The structure (hook, modelling, guided practice, independent paragraph, exit ticket with three levels) is what my university expects.
- No account, and clear, repeated reminders not to enter student names.
- The Grade 10 argumentative prompts in the library were usable as they are, and include a fair prompt on students using AI for writing.

Could I tell it was AI-written? The prompts, no; I could pass those off as a colleague's. The lesson plan, yes, once I checked it: confident, tidy, and wrong about the book in a way no one who had the novel open would be.

## What I could not check

- How anything looks: layout, colours, phone view, how the printout or Word file comes out, and whether ads sit near the document.
- What the Print, Word, Copy, Edit, Share and Revise buttons actually do, including whether Share shows any warning before publishing to the library.
- My documents (it needs a browser with JavaScript).
- Whether my university's AI detection would flag these documents.
- Page-exact wording of the Steinbeck quotations in every edition. I am confident there is no rocking chair or gunny-sack partition in Crooks's room, and confident of the "A guy needs somebody - to be near him" line.

## What I would say to a colleague about this site

It is free, fast and upfront about being AI, and the writing prompts saved me real time. But do not trust it on the actual text of a book: it gave me three "direct quotations" and two were invented, with a note implying only that editions differ. Use it for structure and ideas, paste in your own passages, check every fact, and ask your supervisor before you use it on placement, because the site will not tell you whether you are allowed or how to own up to it. Oddly, if you ask the free-form box to write you a disclosure note, it does that well.
