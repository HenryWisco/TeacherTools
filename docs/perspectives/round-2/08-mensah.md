# Mr. Mensah, English-learner (ESL) teacher with newcomer students

**Verdict:** Would use with conditions.

**In one sentence:** The leveled English and the Spanish are close to classroom-ready and the Haitian Creole sheet did what I asked, but every document had at least one answer-key or instruction problem I would have to catch myself, the text leveler is missing the three-levels and bilingual options I need most, and there is nothing in the library for ESL.

## What I did

Pages visited: home page (/), the tools list (/tools), the Text Leveler page (/tools/text-leveler), the library (/library), the library filtered to ESL / ELL (/library?subject=esl), one library resource (/library/how-honeybees-make-honey-reading-passage-grade-3) and /about. I also looked at the form fields for the text leveler, the reading passage tool and "Ask for Anything".

Documents generated (I wrote my own 190-word water cycle paragraph to level):

1. **08-01-text-leveler.md**: Text Leveler, "English learners (beginner)", "Glossary and questions", language Arabic.
2. **08-02-reading-passage.md**: Reading Passage + Questions, water cycle, grade 6, ESL, Three levels, English + Spanish side by side, asked for a word bank and sentence frames.
3. **08-03-anything.md**: Ask for Anything, a vocabulary and sentence frame sheet for beginner Haitian Creole speakers, language Haitian Creole, with English frames and the Creole translation underneath.

## Problems found

**1. The Text Leveler has no "Three levels" option and no "English + Arabic/Creole side by side" (major, missing feature)**
Where: /tools/text-leveler. The "More options" heading says "versions, country, language, your own text", but the only fields underneath are Country and Language; there is no Versions field. That is the tool I would use most, because I level my own texts for students at three different English levels. The only bilingual choice anywhere is "English + Spanish (side by side)". Nothing like that exists for Arabic or Haitian Creole, so my Arabic and Creole students get either English only or their home language only.
Fix: add the Versions field (three levels) to the leveler, and either let me pick "English + [any language]" side by side or offer a home-language glossary on its own.

**2. Choosing "English learners (beginner)" with Arabic gave a worksheet with no English in it (major, site did not do what was asked / confusing)**
Where: 08-01-text-leveler.md. I picked "Rewrite for: English learners (beginner)". The whole document, including passage, glossary and questions, came out in Arabic. The only English is a stray "(Answer Key)" on the last line. Simplifying Arabic for a "beginner English learner" makes no sense, and a newcomer gets no English practice from it. The form gives no hint that the Language setting overrides the English-learner setting.
Fix: when the target is an English-learner level and the language isn't English, produce simplified English with the home language beside it (or a home-language glossary), or explain on the form what each combination will produce.

**3. Asked for three comprehension questions, got five (minor, site did not do what was asked)**
Where: 08-01-text-leveler.md. The option says "Three comprehension questions" / "Glossary and questions". The document has five numbered questions.
Fix: give the number of questions the option promises.

**4. The Arabic glossary lists words the passage never uses, and one answer is scientifically wrong (major, factual error)**
Where: 08-01-text-leveler.md, Vocabulary table and answer 4. The glossary defines يتبخر (evaporates), الهطول (precipitation) and الجريان السطحي (runoff). The rewritten passage avoids all three: it says "يتحول بعض الماء إلى بخار" and never uses the words for precipitation or runoff. So students are learning words they can't find in the text. In the passage, precipitation is listed as "مطر أو ثلج أو برد" (rain, snow, hail; sleet was dropped). But answer 4 also accepts "الصقيع" (frost), which is not precipitation and is not in the passage. Question 5 asks where groundwater goes, and the answer describes how water becomes groundwater.
Fix: glossary words should appear in the passage, and answer keys should accept only answers the passage supports and that are scientifically correct.

**5. The three-level bilingual passage was cut off before the end, and the first passage has no answers (major, broken)**
Where: 08-02-reading-passage.md. Before the three levels, the document gives a fourth full passage (Parts A–F, English and Spanish, 7 questions each). That passage has no answer key at all. The Spanish answer key then stops mid-sentence at "la precipitación c", followed by: "This document reached the length limit. Ask for a shorter version or fewer questions to get a complete one." I didn't ask for four passages. The unrequested extra one used up the space.
Fix: when three levels are chosen, produce just the three levels (support, on level, challenge), and make sure the answer key fits. If it can't fit, drop the extra content rather than the key.

**6. A Level 2 question can't be answered from the Level 2 passage (major, factual error)**
Where: 08-02-reading-passage.md, Level 2 question 4: "Explain why cold places are more likely to receive snow. Use a detail from the passage." The Level 2 passage says nothing about cold or warm places. That detail is only in the extra base passage and in Level 1. The answer key even says "accept any reasonable detail about cold places in the passage", and there isn't one. The Spanish version has the same problem.
Fix: check that every question is answerable from the passage at its own level.

**7. Grammar error in the Spanish that parents would notice (minor, factual error)**
Where: 08-02-reading-passage.md, Part D last sentence: "El mismo agua se ha estado moviendo…". It should be "La misma agua". "Agua" is feminine; it only takes "el" directly before the noun.
Fix: have the Spanish proofread more carefully for agreement.

**8. The Haitian Creole sheet's answer key is wrong on two of six items (major, factual error)**
Where: 08-03-anything.md, Answer Key.
- Frame 1: "The sun heats the ____ and the water changes into vapor." The key gives "ground" as the answer and only accepts "water" as a "close answer". The answer is "water". "Ground" is wrong.
- Frame 4: "Water collects in ____, lakes, and oceans." The key gives "collection". That is ungrammatical; the answer is "rivers". The key also accepts "lakes", which is already in the sentence.
- Frames 2 and 6 expect "condenses" and "recycled", but the sheet tells students "Ranpli espas vid yo ak mo ki nan Pati 1 la" (fill the blanks with words from Part 1), and neither word is in Part 1.
Fix: make the blanks match the word list, and give one correct answer per blank.

**9. Small instruction mismatches and awkward Creole on the Creole sheet (minor)**
Where: 08-03-anything.md. Part 1 tells students to "Ekri mo kreyòl la ak definisyon an nan pwòp mo ou" (write the Creole word and definition in your own words), but the table is already completely filled in. The heading "Mo kle pou fè lespri ou" reads oddly. Frame 6's Creole "Sik dlo a fè yon bagay ki toujou ap tounen" is clumsy. A Creole-speaking colleague would need to check it before I send it home.
Fix: instructions should match the layout. I'd also like a note on the site saying translations into less common languages should be checked by a speaker.

**10. The ESL / ELL library is empty (major, missing feature)**
Where: /library?subject=esl shows "Nothing here yet for that combination." ESL / ELL is offered as a subject everywhere, but there isn't a single ready-made resource for it. None of the library resources I saw has a home-language version.
Fix: before launch, seed the library with a few checked newcomer resources (survival vocabulary, sentence frames, a leveled passage set in Spanish/Arabic/Creole).

## What worked

- **The three leveled English passages (08-02) are good.** Level 1 has short sentences, four bolded key words, a worked example and sentence starters, which is close to what I would write for a newcomer. Level 2 and Level 3 really are harder and cover the same content. Sentence frames were included at every level, as I asked, and they fit the questions.
- **The Spanish is natural and close to the English line by line** (apart from the one agreement error), with a Spanish answer key where it wasn't cut off. Spanish-speaking parents could follow it.
- **The Arabic in 08-01 reads as correct Modern Standard Arabic.** It was simplified sensibly and kept all the science.
- **The Creole sheet followed a fairly detailed instruction:** English frames for practice with the Creole translation underneath, and an English/Creole/definition table. The Creole word list itself (dlo, nwaj, vapè, dlo anba tè a) looks right.
- All ten home languages I would need for most of my students (Spanish, Arabic, Haitian Creole and others) are in the list, and generation was fast (6–32 seconds).
- The about page and footer say clearly that AI can make mistakes and that documents should be checked, and not to type student names.

## What I could not check

- Whether the Arabic prints right-to-left correctly, and how the tables look on paper or in Word.
- What the "For English learners", "Sentence starters" and "In Spanish" revise buttons do (I used up my three documents).
- Whether the answer key prints on its own page.
- On the library resource I opened, the questions showed as bullets instead of numbers in the text I could see, while the scoring note refers to "questions 1 to 3". This may only be how the helper shows the page.

## What I would say to a colleague about this site

"It's quick, and for Spanish speakers the three-level reading passage gets you most of the way there, with frames at every level. The Arabic and Creole come out readable too. But check every answer key yourself: on mine, two Creole blanks had wrong answers, a question couldn't be answered from its passage, and the long one got cut off before the Spanish key finished. The text leveler can't do three levels or English-plus-Arabic yet, which is what we really need, and there's nothing in the ESL library. Worth a bookmark, not yet something to hand out without reading."
