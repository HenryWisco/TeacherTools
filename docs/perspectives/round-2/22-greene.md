# Mr. Greene, school board member, small-business owner and fiscal conservative

**Verdict:** Would approve with conditions. I would not stop a teacher from using it today, but I would not vote to put the district's name behind it until the site says who runs it, how to reach them, and gets its story straight on how it makes money.

**In one sentence:** The two documents I made were correct and cost the district nothing, but the "who are we and what's the catch" pages are unfinished and disagree with each other, and that is exactly the part a board looks at.

## What I did

Pages read: home (/), /about, /support, /privacy, /terms, /tools, /library, /my, the Quiz tool page, the Family Email tool page, and one library resource (Ratios and Unit Rates Worksheet, Grade 6).

Documents generated (2 of my 3 allowed):

1. `22-01-word-problems.md` - Math Word Problems tool, grade 7, percent (sales tax, markup, discount, simple interest), hardware-store theme, 10 problems. Took about 8 seconds.
2. `22-02-quiz.md` - Quiz tool, grade 12 social studies, how a school district budget works, 10 questions. Took about 11 seconds.

I worked every answer in both documents by hand, and every answer in the library worksheet's key.

## Problems found

### 1. Privacy and Terms pages are visibly unfinished and name no business (blocker)
- Where: bottom of /privacy and bottom of /terms.
- What I saw: "Site owner: review this page with a qualified adviser before launch, and add your business name and address." and on Terms, "Site owner: review this page with a qualified adviser before launch."
- Why it matters: these are notes to the owner, showing to the public. More important, nowhere on the site is there a business name, a person's name, a state, or an address. I cannot tell a parent who is behind this.
- Fix: remove the notes, have the pages reviewed, and state the legal name of the business, where it is based, and an effective date on both pages.

### 2. There is no way to contact anyone (blocker)
- Where: /privacy "Contact" section; also /about, /support, /terms.
- What I saw: "Questions or removal requests: the site owner." No email address, no form, no phone. The Support page says "Tell us what's missing" with no way to tell them. Privacy says "Ask the site owner to remove a shared document at any time" with no way to ask.
- Fix: put a working contact email on Privacy, Terms, About and Support.

### 3. The site tells four different stories about how it is paid for (major)
- Where: home page "Free means free", /about "How it stays free", /support, and the newsletter box in the footer of every page.
- What I saw:
  - Home: "The site is paid for by a few quiet ads and optional supporters."
  - Support: "a few quiet ads cover that."
  - About: "A small number of ads, links to classroom supplies that may earn a commission, an occasional sponsor in the weekly email, and optional supporters."
  - Footer newsletter box: "One email a week. The best new free resources, nothing else." That contradicts "an occasional sponsor in the weekly email."
  - Support also says "Supporter sign-up is coming soon", so the "optional supporters" named on the home page do not exist yet.
- Fix: use one full, identical list of income sources everywhere (ads, Amazon commissions, newsletter sponsors, supporters when available), and change the newsletter line so it does not say "nothing else" if sponsors will appear.

### 4. "Never will" is a promise nobody can check, and there is nothing about what happens if the site closes (major)
- Where: /support, first paragraph.
- What I saw: "Supply Closet has no paywall and never will. Each document costs a fraction of a cent to make, and a few quiet ads cover that." The ad slots I saw are placeholders ("Ad space (home)", "Ad space (tool)"), so no ad money is coming in yet, and there is no statement of who pays the bill if ads fall short.
- Why it matters: I have run a business for a long time. "Free forever" from a one-person shop with no revenue yet is a hope, not a plan. Teachers will build habits on this.
- Fix: replace "never will" with a plain commitment the owner can keep (for example, "generating and printing documents will stay free; if that ever has to change we will give 60 days' notice here"), and add a short "If we shut down" note: your documents are in your browser, so print or save anything you want to keep.

### 5. Privacy page does not say what the AI company does with what teachers type (major)
- Where: /privacy "What we collect", first bullet; home page "Private by design".
- What I saw: "What you type into a tool is sent to our AI provider (Anthropic) to write your document and is not stored by us after the document is returned." Home page says what you make is "saved in your own browser, not on our servers."
- What is missing: how long the AI provider keeps the text, and whether it can be used to train their systems. "Not stored by us" is not the same as "not stored." A teacher will eventually type a child's name no matter how many warnings there are.
- Fix: add one or two sentences stating the provider's retention period and whether submitted text is used for training, with a link to the provider's terms.

### 6. Daily limits are mentioned but never stated, and a school building may share one limit (major, could not test)
- Where: /terms "Fair use" and /privacy "daily usage counter".
- What I saw: "Daily limits apply to keep the site free for everyone." and the counter is tied to "a scrambled, one-day version of your network address." No number is given anywhere.
- Why it matters: every teacher in a school building usually goes out to the internet through the same network address. If the limit is counted per address, forty teachers could use up one allowance by second period. I could not test this, but the pages do not rule it out.
- Fix: state the daily limit in plain numbers on the Terms page and say how it works for many teachers on one school network.

### 7. Terms are thin for something a district would be asked to approve (major, policy gap)
- Where: /terms.
- What I saw: seven bullet points. No effective date, no statement of how changes to the terms will be announced, no governing state, and no limit on liability beyond "provided as is, without warranty."
- Fix: add an effective date, a "how we'll tell you about changes" line, the governing state, and a standard limitation-of-liability paragraph, as part of the adviser review the page already says is needed.

### 8. Advertising cookies from Google on a tool used on school computers (minor to major depending on your state, policy gap)
- Where: /privacy "Cookies and advertising".
- What I saw: "Ads are shown without personalization... Our advertising partners, including Google, may still use cookies to deliver and measure ads."
- What I liked: ads are kept off printed documents and off the family message, report card, IEP and differentiation tools. I confirmed the Family Email page shows no ad slot while the Quiz page does.
- What is missing: no mention of a cookie notice or choice, and no one-page summary a technology director could hand to a board.
- Fix: add a short "For schools and districts" page: what is collected, what is not, who the ad and AI vendors are, and a contact for questions.

### 9. Family Email tool offers revision buttons that make no sense for an email (minor, confusing)
- Where: /tools/parent-email, under "Want it different?".
- What I saw: buttons "Easier", "Harder", "Version B", "Sentence starters", "For English learners", "More questions", and the hint "add 5 harder questions". Those belong to worksheets and quizzes, not a letter home. The "More options" line also reads "versions, country, language, your own text" although this tool has no versions choice and Language is already shown above it.
- Fix: on the Family Email (and likely Report Card Comments) tools, show email-appropriate buttons only, such as Shorter, More formal, Warmer, In Spanish.

### 10. Shopping links on the library page do not match the resource (minor)
- Where: /library/ratios-and-unit-rates-worksheet-grade-6, "Handy for this lesson".
- What I saw: "Fraction tiles & circles", "Dry-erase boards, class set", "Base ten blocks" on a grade 6 ratios and unit rates worksheet. Base ten blocks are an early-elementary item. The Amazon disclosure itself is present and clear, which I appreciate.
- Fix: match product links to the grade and topic, or drop the section where nothing fits. A site that tells me teachers "already spend hundreds of dollars a year of their own money" should be careful about nudging them to spend more.

### 11. Small quality notes on the two documents (minor)
- `22-01-word-problems.md`, problem 10: it is two unrelated problems stuck together (a loan, then a lawn mower sale). I asked for multi-step problems; this is two one-step problems in one paragraph. Problem 9's wording is clumsy ("Then the store charges 8% sales tax on each sale of the remaining flashlights"). All ten answers are correct.
- `22-01-word-problems.md`: directions say "Show your work in the space provided" but each problem is followed by a single line. I could not see the printed layout, so this may look different on paper.
- `22-02-quiz.md`, question 9 and its key: correct as worded, but in many states the law automatically rolls the rate back after a reassessment, so "the board keeps the rate the same and revenue rises 20 percent" is not how it works everywhere. The key mentions exemptions and appeals but not this. Question 7's key does say "Accept answers that note state-specific rules", which is the right instinct. There is no place on the form to pick a state.
- Fix: for civics and government topics, have the answer key add a "check your own state's rules" note wherever tax law differs by state.

## What worked

- Both documents were accurate. I checked all 10 word-problem answers and all 5 multiple-choice answers and the mill-rate arithmetic ($800 million x 2.5 mills = $2,000,000) and found no wrong answers. The library worksheet's 15-item key was also correct.
- I got what I asked for: 10 problems, at least two simple interest problems (6, 8, 10), sales tax, markup and discount all covered, hardware-store theme, sensible prices. The quiz had 10 questions and the points added up to the 30 stated.
- The school-budget quiz was better than I expected. The explanation of why bonds suit a 40-year building and the declining-enrollment question are things I wish more voters understood.
- Speed: 8 and 11 seconds.
- No sign-up really means no sign-up. I was never asked for an email to get a document.
- Plain disclosure of Amazon commissions on the library page, and ads kept away from the sensitive tools and off printed pages.
- The warning not to enter student names appears on every page and right beside the boxes where a teacher types.
- Terms say clearly that teachers may print, edit and share what they make, free of charge.

## What I could not check

- What real ads look like, how many there are, or what they advertise. Every ad slot is a placeholder.
- Whether "a fraction of a cent" per document is true, and whether ads would actually cover it.
- The daily limit number and what happens when it is reached.
- Print, Word, Copy for Google Docs, Large print and Share buttons; the look of the page; phone use.
- My documents page (it only showed "Loading...").
- Whether the usage counter and "we don't keep what you type" claims are true behind the scenes. I can only read what the pages say.
- Who reviews library resources and what qualifies them. The site says "reviewed before being listed" but not by whom.

## What I would say to a colleague about this site

The product is honestly good for the price, which is zero: I asked for a percent worksheet and a civics quiz on school budgets and got two correct, usable documents in under fifteen seconds each. The catch, as far as I can find, is ordinary: ads, Amazon commissions and a sponsored newsletter, and no student data is supposed to go in. What worries me is not a hidden cost but the lack of a name on the door. There is no business name, no address, no email, the legal pages still have the owner's to-do notes on them, and the "free forever" promise comes from one person with no income from it yet. If a teacher wants to use it tonight, fine, read the answer key first. If the superintendent asks the board to recommend it district-wide, my answer is "come back when the privacy and terms pages are finished and there is someone to call."
