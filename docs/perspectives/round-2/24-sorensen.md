# Ms. Sorensen, school board member and retired teacher of 35 years
**Verdict:** Would approve with conditions.
**In one sentence:** A sound skeleton a seasoned teacher could fix in ten minutes, but it contains plain mistakes about the book and a missing quotation, and a new teacher would not spot them.

## What I did
- Pages visited: home, /tools, /about, /privacy, /terms, /library (list only; I did not open individual resources).
- Documents generated (Grade 10 English, Of Mice and Men, mixed-ability class of 28):
  - Lesson plan, 50 min: samples/24-01-lesson-plan.md
  - Unit plan, 3 weeks: samples/24-02-unit-plan.md

## Problems found
1. **Lesson plan, Step 4: the key quotation is missing (major).** It says "Show this line from the chapter (paraphrase is acceptable...):" and then nothing follows. The whole foreshadowing step depends on it. Fix: either quote an actual line (copyright-safe short quote) or say plainly "choose a line, for example the one where ..." and name it.
2. **Lesson plan, Step 2: wrong about the text (major).** It says the first two paragraphs "describe the Salinas River, the wild land, and the bunkhouse". Chapter 1 never visits a bunkhouse; the men camp in a clearing by the river. The bunkhouse is chapter 2. Fix: do not name locations the model cannot be sure of.
3. **Lesson plan, extension task: wrong about the text (major).** "Steinbeck uses a dog and a rabbit in this chapter." There is no dog in chapter 1 (Candy's dog is chapter 2, and its shooting is chapter 3). Lennie's dead mouse is the animal in chapter 1; the rabbits are only imagined. A student would be sent hunting for something that is not there.
4. **Lesson plan, Hook (minor to major).** "Two men walk into a place where they are not welcome" does not describe chapter 1. They are walking to a job, and nobody is hostile to them. It sends predictions the wrong way.
5. **Lesson plan, pacing is not realistic (major).** In 50 minutes: hook, close reading with 3 questions, reading and a three-row chart, three discussion questions, a mini-lesson, sticky notes, a 5-7 sentence written response, and an exit slip. Chapter 1 is about a dozen pages; silent reading by 28 mixed-ability students will not fit in 22 minutes. A veteran would cut half of it. Fix: state honestly that reading happens before class or take two periods.
6. **Lesson plan, closing is duplicated (minor).** Step 7 asks for a one-sentence exit slip, then "Formative Assessment" asks for a different two-question exit slip. The teacher has to guess which.
7. **Unit plan, Day 2: wrong about the text (major).** "Read Ch. 1 to the arrival of George and Lennie at the ranch." They arrive at the ranch in chapter 2. Chapter 1 ends at the river camp.
8. **Unit plan, big omission (major).** The shooting of Candy's dog (chapter 3), which is the central piece of foreshadowing for the ending, is never mentioned in the day-by-day plan. Slim is also absent. Chapters 3 and 4 share one day, yet Chapter 3 contains the long Candy/dream scene and the fight. The plan spends nine of fifteen periods reading in class, and then rushes the middle.
9. **Unit plan, citation (minor).** It calls "Steinbeck, ch. 3" MLA style. MLA would give a page number. Also "the crooked hand" is listed as a symbol; Crooks has a crooked back and Curley's hand is crushed. Unclear what is meant.
10. **Unit plan, quiz promised but not provided (minor).** A reading quiz is scheduled and the answer key just says "answers are provided by the teacher". A real key was expected.
11. **Answer keys are vague (minor).** Much of the lesson key is "accept any reasonable answer". Fine for open questions, but a new teacher gets no model response to compare against.

## What worked
- The structure is sensible: a clear objective list, a gradual release of responsibility, partner talk, evidence demanded ("Where do you see that in the text?"), and a concrete exit check.
- The misconceptions list in the unit plan is genuinely good (summary versus analysis, "George is a bad friend", handling slurs directly). That is craft knowledge.
- The model refused to invent a standard code and said so, rather than guessing. The disclaimers (About, Terms, footer) are plain: AI makes errors, check before you teach.
- Privacy page is honest and readable.

## What I could not check
- Layout, print appearance, the Print/Word buttons, phone use.
- Whether library resources are really reviewed; I only read the list and descriptions.

## What I would say to a colleague about this site
Does it deskill new teachers? Partly. The danger is not that it plans for them, it is that it plans confidently and wrongly, and an inexperienced teacher who has not read the book will not catch "the bunkhouse in chapter 1" or the dog that is not there. The plan also hides the real craft decisions: why this passage, what students will get wrong, how long reading actually takes. The experienced teacher will use it as a first draft, which is fine. For new teachers I would want a visible line on every plan: "This was written by AI and has not been checked against your text. Read the chapter first." The site's disclaimers exist but sit in the footer; they should be on the document. As a board member I would approve it as a drafting aid, not as a replacement for planning, and I would not want it presented to new teachers as finished lesson plans.
