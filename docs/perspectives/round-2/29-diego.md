# Diego, bilingual elementary student teacher and career changer, age 38

**Verdict:** Would use with conditions.

**In one sentence:** The Spanish is mostly good and the family email was nearly ready to send, but "side by side" isn't side by side, the kindergarten material asks pre-readers to "circle the picture" when there are no pictures, and as someone who used to vet vendors for a living I couldn't find a single way to contact the people running this.

## What I did

- **Pages visited:** home (/), /tools, /library, the library filtered to K–2, ESL/ELL and World Languages, a library search for "spanish", the kindergarten resource "Counting and Comparing Numbers to 10 Worksheet", /about, /privacy, /terms, /support, /my. I also looked at the forms for the Family Email, Vocabulary List and Reading Passage tools.
- **Documents generated (3):**
  1. `29-01-vocabulary.md`: Vocabulary List, Grade 1 science, "Parts of a plant and what plants need to grow", 10 words, language "English + Spanish (side by side)". In the notes I asked for cognates and very short sentences.
  2. `29-02-reading-passage.md`: Reading Passage, Kindergarten ELA, fiction, about 150 words, a family growing a bean seed, "English + Spanish (side by side)", standard TEKS K.6B. In the notes I said it would be read aloud and asked for 4 questions students answer by drawing or circling.
  3. `29-03-parent-email.md`: Family Email in Spanish, warm tone. The email covered supplies for a plant unit plus an invitation to Family Literacy Night, signed by me as student teacher with my lead teacher.

## Problems found

1. **"Side by side" isn't side by side.** *Major.* Both bilingual documents (`29-01`, `29-02`) give the full English section and then the full Spanish section ("### English" ... then "### Español"). In the vocabulary list that means two separate 10-row tables. In a dual-language classroom, side by side means a column for each language, so a child or parent can match "root" to "raíz" on the same line. As I saw it, the label promises a layout the document doesn't have. (I could only see the document text, so if the print view shows columns, ignore this. The text itself is stacked.) **Fix:** for the side-by-side option, use one table with English and Spanish columns (Word | Palabra | Definition | Definición), or two-column pages. Otherwise rename the option "English, then Spanish".

2. **The kindergarten worksheet refers to pictures that don't exist.** *Major.* In `29-02`, question 1 says "Who plants the bean seed? Circle the picture." The choices are the words "(a) Mia (b) a dog (c) a bird", and there are no pictures. Question 3 asks five-year-olds to circle "a small green plant that comes up from the soil". The directions say "Read the questions" even though I told it the teacher reads aloud. The vocabulary sheet (`29-01`) has the same problem: "Draw a line from each word to the correct picture description" has no pictures, only written definitions. **Fix:** for K–1, either leave placeholder boxes like "[picture: a girl]" for the teacher to fill, or write questions that don't depend on images. Also change "Read the questions" to "Listen as your teacher reads" when the request says read-aloud.

3. **No contact information anywhere on the site.** *Major (for trust).* Privacy says "Questions or removal requests: the site owner." Support says "Tell us what's missing." Neither gives an email, form or name. Privacy also says you can "Ask the site owner to remove a shared document at any time" without saying how. In my old job a vendor with no contact, no company name and no address would fail vetting at step one. My district tech coordinator would ask the same. **Fix:** add a contact email or form, the operator's name or business name, and a mailing address to Privacy, About and the footer.

4. **Notes meant for the site owner show on the public legal pages.** *Major (for trust).* At the bottom of /privacy: "Site owner: review this page with a qualified adviser before launch, and add your business name and address." At the bottom of /terms: "Site owner: review this page with a qualified adviser before launch." Anyone reading those pages sees that the legal text hasn't been reviewed. **Fix:** do the review and remove both lines before launch.

5. **My cognates request was ignored.** *Minor.* In `29-01` I wrote "Please include cognates where they exist." The list has clear cognate pairs (plant/planta, flower/flor, air/aire) and none are marked. Cognates are a core strategy in dual-language teaching. **Fix:** follow requests in "Anything else", and consider marking cognates automatically when the language is English + Spanish.

6. **My job title is mistranslated in the parent email.** *Minor.* `29-03` signs me as "Mr. Diego, estudiante de maestro". That isn't natural Spanish, and Spanish-speaking parents would notice. The usual terms are "maestro practicante" or "maestro en formación". The rest of the email was very good. **Fix:** check the Spanish rendering of common school roles (student teacher, lead teacher, para, counselor) so they come out as maestro practicante, maestra titular, etc.

7. **The reading passage came out at about half the length I picked.** *Minor.* I chose "About 150 words". The English story in `29-02` is about 88 words. For kindergarten, shorter is arguably better, but the length setting should do what it says, or say it adjusts for grade. **Fix:** hit the chosen length, or tell the user "shortened for Kindergarten".

8. **The Family Email tool's language field works differently from every other tool.** *Minor.* On Vocabulary and Reading Passage, Language is a dropdown that includes "English + Spanish (side by side)". On Family Email it's a free-text box ("English (or e.g. English + Spanish)"). Family emails are where I need bilingual output most, so it's the tool where this matters most. **Fix:** use the same dropdown, and put "English + Spanish" near the top.

9. **No bilingual or Spanish material in the library.** *Minor.* The ESL/ELL and World Languages filters both say "Nothing here yet for that combination." Searching "spanish" returns nothing, and the K–2 band has only 3 items. The kindergarten counting worksheet I opened shows "Handy for this lesson" links to fraction tiles and base ten blocks, and its "More like this" lists grade 4–9 resources. **Fix:** add a few bilingual K–2 resources before launch, and match affiliate and "more like this" links to the grade.

Smaller things I noticed:
- The bilingual documents print "Name / Date" only in English. Use "Name / Nombre" and "Date / Fecha".
- The vocabulary list gives first graders a "Part of speech" column, which they don't need.
- The matching answers in `29-01` follow an easy pattern (B, C, D, A, E) that kids can guess.

## What worked

- **The Spanish is mostly correct and natural.** It reads like real classroom Spanish for a US audience ("frijol", "encierra en un círculo", "maestra titular", "hijo o hija"). The vocabulary definitions are simple and parallel in both languages.
- **The family email (`29-03`) was close to ready.** It had a clear subject line, the dates and times I gave were all correct, the supplies were bulleted, and the sibling childcare was mentioned. After fixing the one title, I'd send it.
- **The kindergarten passage reads well aloud.** It has short sentences and a gentle story arc, and the Spanish parallels the English closely. The answer key comes in both languages.
- **Answer keys were correct.** I checked every matching item and comprehension answer. The library counting worksheet's 15 items and key were also correct.
- **Clear privacy promises.** No account, documents stay in your browser, and repeated warnings not to enter student names. The ad policy says no ads beside the family message tool, which I appreciated.
- **Fast:** 3 to 7 seconds per document.

## What I could not check

- Whether the printed or Word version lays the two languages out as columns. I only saw the text.
- How it looks on a phone, colors, and what the Print, Word, Copy for Google Docs, "In Spanish" and Revise buttons actually do.
- Whether accents (á, é, ñ, ¿, ¡) survive Word download and Google Docs paste.

## What I would say to a colleague about this site

"It's a real time-saver for bilingual drafts, and the Spanish is better than most free tools I've tried. The family email was nearly send-ready. But read every K–1 page first: it'll tell five-year-olds to 'circle the picture' when there isn't one, and 'side by side' actually means English first, then Spanish. I wouldn't bring it to our tech coordinator yet, though. There's no contact info and no company name, and the privacy page still has a note telling the owner to get it reviewed. I'd want that fixed before asking the school to approve it."
