# Mr. Haddad, high school US history and civics teacher
**Verdict:** Would use with conditions. I would not hand any of these out without a line-by-line check against the real sources.

**In one sentence:** The tone is careful and the neutrality advice is good, but the Civil War lesson prints "quotations" from the secession declarations that I could not trust, one document was cut off mid-table, and the immigration questions are lopsided in one place. In my district those are exactly the mistakes that end up in front of a board.

## What I did
- Pages visited: home (/), /tools, /library, /about, /privacy. I also looked at the forms for lesson-plan, discussion-questions and reading-passage. The library page listed a Civil Rights primary-source lesson and a Causes of WWI quiz, but I did not open any library resource.
- Documents generated (3):
  1. `06-01-lesson-plan.md`: Causes of the Civil War, grade 11, 50 minutes, with primary sources and historians' interpretations.
  2. `06-02-discussion-questions.md`: US immigration policy (border enforcement, legal immigration levels, DACA), grade 12 civics, strictly nonpartisan.
  3. `06-03-reading-passage.md`: Reconstruction, about 500 words, grade 11, with five questions and a key.
- I checked facts from my own knowledge of the period. I had no outside reference books open, so where I say "I could not confirm" I mean that.

## Problems found

1. **Secession "quotations" look unreliable, and the document admits it.** Where: 06-01, Source A1 (South Carolina) and A2 (Mississippi).
   - Saw: the excerpts are stitched together with ellipses. A1 has South Carolina saying the other section's citizens are "the exclusive advocates of negro equality" and that slaveholders "have been denounced as the sinners." A2 ends with "Our decision is made, and we have only to fear that the ensuing calamity will not be as severe as we know it will be," and includes "we, too, have heard the sound of the approaching conflict in the blast of the drum."
   - I recognise "Our position is thoroughly identified with the institution of slavery, the greatest material interest of the world" and "Argument is exhausted" as real Mississippi language. I could not match the other lines to the real documents.
   - The sheet tells the teacher "Verify wording against your preferred edition before printing" and says the excerpt "reflects the document's language."
   - Severity: **blocker** for a divided district. A misquoted primary source on the causes of the Civil War is the worst case.
   - Fix: only use exact, verbatim excerpts from the Avalon Project or Library of Congress text. If the tool cannot guarantee exact quotes, it should name the document and section and tell the teacher to paste in the text, not invent it. It should not present reconstructed lines inside quotation marks.

2. **The lesson plan was cut off.** Where: end of 06-01, in the Exit Ticket Rubric table, which stops at "Uses one source".
   - The site prints "This document reached the length limit. Ask for a shorter version." I had asked for one 50-minute lesson.
   - Severity: **major**. A teacher who prints this gets a half-finished rubric.
   - Fix: raise the length limit, or cut the output automatically so the document is always complete, including the rubric. At minimum, put the warning at the top of the page, not the bottom.

3. **Historian summaries are labelled "paraphrased," but they put strong claims in real scholars' mouths.** Where: 06-01, Source Set B.
   - Foner, Beard and McPherson are summarised and given an expected "Leaves out" answer. For example, the key says Foner "leaves out economic disputes as a separate driver."
   - The sheet tells the teacher to confirm the wording against the books.
   - Severity: **major**. Attributing positions to named living or recent historians is risky if the summary is off.
   - Fix: describe the schools of thought (slavery-centred, economic, states'-rights/constitutional) without naming people unless the teacher supplies the text. Or give titles only and no claimed arguments.

4. **The "balance" is lopsided on the causes of the Civil War.** Where: 06-01, Source Set A and B.
   - There is no primary source that argues the states'-rights position, such as Jefferson Davis or the Texas declaration. There is no historian who argues it either.
   - States' rights appears only in a differentiation extension: "Challenge them to find a historian who argues that states' rights... was the primary cause."
   - Step 2 also says "Slavery was the central issue" as a flat statement, then tells students "Historians disagree about which cause mattered most."
   - Severity: **major** for my district. A parent who holds the states'-rights view will say the lesson only gives the other side.
   - Fix: when the teacher asks for balance, include the strongest source for each major interpretation, and say plainly which view the evidence supports and which the evidence has mostly rejected. The tool should not both state a conclusion and claim there is open disagreement.

5. **Immigration questions are asymmetric in one place.** Where: 06-02, Understand Q1 and Q2.
   - Q1 asks what enforcement supporters say, but Q2 asks only for reasons to support a "more open or expanded" legal system. Nothing asks for the case for reduced or more selective legal immigration.
   - I asked for the strongest version of both sides.
   - Severity: **major**. This is the kind of detail a board member would notice.
   - Fix: pair every question. Q2 should ask for both the case for expanding and the case for reducing legal immigration.

6. **The immigration sources list leans one way.** Where: 06-02, Teacher Notes.
   - Federal data sources (CBP, DHS, CRS, USCIS) are fine and good.
   - Migration Policy Institute and Pew are described as publishing "from different perspectives." Both are generally seen as centrist or centre-left. There is no restrictionist think tank listed, such as the Center for Immigration Studies.
   - Severity: **minor to major**.
   - Fix: list at least one source from each side, or drop the claim that the two named sources differ in perspective.

7. **The terminology instruction is itself contested.** Where: 06-02, Neutrality guidelines.
   - The sheet says to use "undocumented immigrants" rather than "illegals." Many on one side regard "undocumented" as a loaded term too. The sheet does say to discuss why word choice matters, which helps.
   - Severity: **minor**.
   - Fix: offer a neutral phrase such as "people present without legal authorization," or list the terms each side uses and let the teacher choose.

8. **Reconstruction passage is loose on numbers, and it overshoots the length.** Where: 06-03.
   - "Between 1869 and 1901, more than 2,000 Black men held public office in the South and nationally." The commonly cited figure of about 2,000 is for the Reconstruction era. 1901 is the end of Black service in Congress, so the date range does not fit the count.
   - "withdrew federal protection... for nearly a century" is a strong simplification.
   - "Thousands of churches" and "literacy... from nearly zero" have no source.
   - The passage is well over the 500 words I asked for. I estimate it runs around 600, but I did not count it exactly.
   - Severity: **major** for the 2,000 figure and the date range. **Minor** for the rest.
   - Fix: say "during Reconstruction" or give an exact, sourced count. Keep to the requested length.

9. **Reconstruction passage leaves out major topics and the answer to Q4 is weak.** Where: 06-03.
   - It does not mention the Black Codes, Andrew Johnson, sharecropping, or Reconstruction's land policy.
   - The Q4 key accepts "This 'Dunning School' view shaped popular memory for decades," which does not show a disagreement between historians by itself.
   - Severity: **minor**.
   - Fix: tighten the Q4 key so the sentence chosen must show two views. Add the Black Codes in one line.

10. **No sources are cited anywhere except by instruction.** Where: all three documents.
    - I asked for sourcing. The Civil War lesson names two websites in a teacher note. The Reconstruction passage has no sources at all. The immigration sheet has a source list but no figures.
    - Severity: **major**.
    - Fix: add a short "Sources and further reading" box to every history and civics document, and say when a claim has no source.

## What worked
- The neutrality guidance in the immigration document is the most useful thing I saw. The think-pair-share routine, the "explain the strongest argument for a position you may not hold" first round, and "never ask students to share family immigration status" are things I would copy.
- The Civil War lesson is well organised and timed to 50 minutes. The Hook, Direct Instruction, Source Analysis and Exit Ticket steps are sensible. The exit ticket asks students to acknowledge one argument against their own view.
- Timeline dates in the Civil War document were correct as far as I checked (Missouri Compromise 1820, Dred Scott 1857, six states seceding by February 1861, Sumter on 12 April).
- Reconstruction amendment dates, Freedmen's Bureau 1865, Revels in 1870 and the 1877 compromise were correct. It named the Dunning School and Du Bois accurately.
- The site tells teachers on every page to review before use, and the About page says AI can make mistakes. That is honest.

## What I could not check
- How anything looks or prints, the Print/Word/Edit/Share buttons, and mobile layout.
- Whether the library resources are as reviewed as advertised. I did not open one, and I did not read the Terms or Support pages.
- Exact wording of the secession declarations and the exact word count of the passage. I had no reference text open.

## What I would say to a colleague about this site
"It is a fast first draft, not a finished handout. It handled the politics of immigration more carefully than I expected, and it never lectured the kids. But on the Civil War it gave me quotation marks around words I could not confirm, and it ran out of room before finishing a rubric. If you teach anything touchy, paste in your own source text, check every quote, and read the whole thing before it goes in front of students. I would not hand it to a substitute without checking it first."
