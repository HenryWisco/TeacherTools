# Ms. Kowalski, special education teacher (self-contained classroom)

**Verdict:** Would use with conditions. I would use the differentiation tool and the text leveler now. I would not recommend the IEP goal tool to my team until the student-name problem and the goal-quality problems below are fixed.

**In one sentence:** The site tells me in the right places not to type student details, but when I typed a (made-up) first name anyway it printed it in the title of the IEP document without a word, and the goals it wrote are measurable but have no timeframe and include one goal for a skill the student already has.

## What I did

Pages visited: home, /tools, /tools/iep-goals, /tools/accommodations (Differentiation Ideas), /tools/text-leveler, /privacy, /terms, /about, /my, /library, a library search for "money", and the library resource "Counting and Comparing Numbers to 10 Worksheet" (Kindergarten).

Documents generated (3):

1. `samples/07-01-iep-goals.md` - IEP Goal Ideas, Grade 5, US. I described functional math (coins to 50 cents at 40% accuracy, identifies all four coins, uses a calculator for single-digit addition) and out-of-seat behavior (about 8 times per 30-minute lesson, 3+ verbal prompts). I deliberately started the description with an invented first name, "Jayden", to see what the site would do.
2. `samples/07-02-text-leveler.md` - Text Leveler. I pasted a 260-word passage I wrote on honeybees and pollination, target "Grade 1-2", extras "Glossary and questions".
3. `samples/07-03-accommodations.md` - Differentiation Ideas, Grade 4 science, for the same honeybee article plus a five-sentence paragraph. I described my class (8 students, pre-primer to grade 2 readers, two AAC users, one student with low vision, fine motor needs, two paraprofessionals, nobody ready for grade-level extension).

## Problems found

### 1. A student name I typed was printed in the document title, with no warning or removal
- **Where:** IEP Goal Ideas tool, sample 07-01, title line.
- **What I saw:** The title came back as "IEP Goal Ideas: Jayden, Grade 5". The form says "Never enter a student's name, ID, diagnosis details or anything that could identify them", which is good, but nothing stopped me, warned me after I pressed the button, or stripped the name out. It was sent to the AI company and came back on the printable page (and, per the site, is saved in the browser under My documents - on a shared classroom computer that matters).
- **How serious:** Major. Tired teachers will type "Jayden reads at..." out of habit. A written warning is not protection.
- **Fix:** On the IEP, differentiation, report comment and family email tools, check the box before sending. If it looks like a person's name is there ("Jayden is...", "my student Maria"), stop and ask me to replace it with "the student". At the very least, tell the AI never to repeat names and always write "the student".

### 2. No timeframe in any annual goal
- **Where:** Sample 07-01, Goal Options A, B and C.
- **What I saw:** Each goal has Condition, Observable behavior, Criterion and How it will be measured. None says "By [date]" or "within 36 instructional weeks". Example: "With 80% accuracy across 4 out of 5 consecutive data sessions" - by when?
- **How serious:** Major. An annual goal with no "by when" is the first thing a compliance reviewer or a parent's advocate circles.
- **Fix:** Start every goal with a blank the team fills in, e.g. "By ____ (annual review date), given...". Do the same for the short-term objectives ("By the end of the first reporting period...").

### 3. One of the three goals is for a skill the student already has
- **Where:** Sample 07-01, "Annual Goal Option B: Calculator Use for Single-Digit Addition".
- **What I saw:** I wrote that he "Uses a calculator for single-digit addition" as a present strength. The tool turned that into a year-long goal: enter single-digit problems in a calculator "With 90% accuracy". There is no baseline number for it and nothing to show it is a need.
- **How serious:** Major. A goal that is not tied to a need and is not ambitious is exactly the kind of goal that causes trouble later. A new teacher might paste it straight in.
- **Fix:** Only write goals for needs that have a baseline. Treat things the teacher lists as "can do" as strengths to build on (for example, a next-step goal: use the calculator to add prices to $1.00), and say in the document why each goal follows from the present level.

### 4. Short-term objectives are out of order and some cannot really be measured
- **Where:** Sample 07-01, objectives under all three goals.
- **What I saw:**
  - Goal A objective 3: "Given a printed coin-value chart, the student counts the total with 80% accuracy, then without the chart with 70% accuracy." That is two targets in one objective, the support (the chart) arrives last instead of first, it ends at 70% when the annual goal is 80%, and it has no "across how many sessions".
  - Goal B objective 3 adds "a visual cue card" after objective 2 already has the student working "independently".
  - Goal C objective 3: "uses a break card ... on at least 3 occasions per lesson, when needed". "At least 3" and "when needed" contradict each other; it would count as failure on a day he does not need a break.
  - Goal C objective 1 mixes two measures: "remains seated for 10 consecutive minutes ... with no more than 4 unauthorized departures per lesson".
- **How serious:** Major. These are the parts I would have to rewrite completely.
- **Fix:** One behavior and one criterion per objective; objectives should step from most support to least and end at the annual criterion; every objective needs a number of sessions. For replacement behaviors use a percentage of opportunities ("uses the break card instead of leaving on 80% of occasions he leaves his seat").

### 5. The IEP document carries a student "Name" line and an empty "Answer Key"
- **Where:** Sample 07-01, top and bottom. The Name line is also on the differentiation document (07-03), which is a teacher document.
- **What I saw:** "**Name:** ______  **Date:** ______" right under the title, and at the end "## Answer Key - Not applicable. This document contains no questions or problems for students."
- **How serious:** Minor, but it looks careless on a legal-adjacent document and a Name line invites people to write the child's name on a draft.
- **Fix:** Remove the Name/Date line and the Answer Key section from IEP Goal Ideas and Differentiation Ideas.

### 6. The disclaimer inside the IEP document is one soft sentence
- **Where:** Sample 07-01, first paragraph.
- **What I saw:** "These are draft ideas for the IEP team to adapt, since goals must be individualized by the team..." The Terms page says more ("not legal or clinical advice"), but that is not on the page I print. Nothing mentions state or district requirements, or that the behavior goal should be based on why the behavior happens (a functional behavior assessment).
- **How serious:** Minor.
- **Fix:** Put a short boxed note at the top of every IEP document: draft only, not legal advice, check your state and district rules, do not copy without team review, and for behavior goals consider the function of the behavior first.

### 7. The "Want it different?" buttons on the IEP page do not fit
- **Where:** /tools/iep-goals and /tools/accommodations.
- **What I saw:** Buttons "Easier", "Harder", "Version B", "Sentence starters", "More questions" under an IEP goal document.
- **How serious:** Minor. Confusing rather than harmful.
- **Fix:** On the IEP tool offer things like "More goal options", "Add a baseline line", "Lower the criterion", "Fewer objectives".

### 8. Text leveler: "Grade 1-2" came back closer to grade 3, with a glossary word that is not in the text
- **Where:** Sample 07-02.
- **What I saw:** Sentences such as "The way the bee turns shows which way to fly, compared to the sun" and "A healthy bee home in summer can have 20,000 to 60,000 bees", and words like "shallow", "pebbles", "naturally", "blooming". About 280 words in seven paragraphs. My grade 1-2 readers could not read this alone. The glossary defines "Pollinator", but that word never appears in the rewritten passage. The "Queen" entry says "The one female bee in a hive who lays the eggs" straight after the passage says almost all bees are female, which will confuse literal readers.
- **How serious:** Minor to major depending on the class. The facts all matched my original, which I appreciate.
- **Fix:** For Grade 1-2, cap sentence length and passage length, drop the hardest detail rather than keep everything, and only put words in the glossary that are in the passage. An "even simpler / shorter" option for pre-primer and picture-supported text would make this useful in a self-contained room.

### 9. Text leveler gave five questions when the form implies three
- **Where:** Sample 07-02; the form's "Add" choices are "Three comprehension questions" and "Glossary and questions".
- **What I saw:** I chose "Glossary and questions" and got five questions. Question 1, "What is the bee carrying from flower to flower?", could fairly be answered "nectar" but the key only accepts "Pollen".
- **How serious:** Minor.
- **Fix:** Rename the option "Glossary and three questions" and give three, or let me choose a number. Accept "nectar" or reword question 1.

### 10. Differentiation document mixes up AAC users and English learners, and adds a section I did not need
- **Where:** Sample 07-03, "English learners" section, item 7.
- **What I saw:** I did not mention any English learners. The section appeared anyway and says "Let students respond in their first language or with AAC before English." Non-speaking students who use AAC are not English learners; AAC is their voice, not a step before English.
- **How serious:** Minor, but it is a real mistake and one that upsets families.
- **Fix:** Only include an English learners section when the teacher mentions them (or label it "if you have English learners"), and never group AAC with home languages.

### 11. Smaller slips in the differentiation document
- **Where:** Sample 07-03.
- **What I saw:**
  - Item 14 sort: "bee, flower, apple, honey, cow, car" into "bees help" and "bees do not help" - honey and cow have no clear right answer and no key is given.
  - Level B table, step 1: the "Response" column says "Paraprofessional reads aloud", which is not a student response; step 4 says "Write or dictate three sentences" with the response "Use stickers, stamps...".
  - Item 2: "hold up a sticky note of yellow dust to represent pollen" - unclear what that is.
  - The student with low vision gets one line at the very end, while almost every idea relies on small picture cards.
  - It never says which changes are accommodations (same expectation, different access) and which are modifications (changed expectation, e.g. pointing to three pictures instead of writing a paragraph). That difference matters for IEPs and grading.
- **How serious:** Minor.
- **Fix:** Add a key for any sorting task, fix the table columns, give low-vision adaptations for each picture-based idea (enlarged, high contrast, real objects), and label each idea "accommodation" or "modification".

### 12. Privacy page is unfinished and does not answer what my district would ask
- **Where:** /privacy and /terms.
- **What I saw:** Visible notes to the owner: "Site owner: review this page with a qualified adviser before launch, and add your business name and address." Contact is given only as "the site owner" with no email. The page says what I type "is sent to our AI provider (Anthropic)" and "not stored by us", but not whether or how long the AI company keeps it. The home page says "nothing is collected about students", which is only true if every teacher follows the instruction.
- **How serious:** Major for launch. I could not get this approved by my district as it stands.
- **Fix:** Finish the page: a real contact address, who runs the site, what the AI provider keeps and for how long, and a plain statement of what happens if a teacher types a student's name by mistake.

### 13. Nothing in the library for special education
- **Where:** /library; search "money".
- **What I saw:** No special education, life skills or functional academics category; the search for "money" returned "Nothing here yet for that combination." On the kindergarten counting worksheet, the "Handy for this lesson" shopping links were "Fraction tiles & circles" and "Base ten blocks", which do not match counting to 10.
- **How serious:** Minor (missing content rather than an error).
- **Fix:** Add a few functional-skills resources (coin counting, telling time, visual schedules) and match the shopping links to the resource.

## What worked

- The warning on the IEP form is clear and in the right place: "Describe the need in general terms only. Never enter a student's name, ID, diagnosis details or anything that could identify them." The same message is in the footer of every page, in the Terms and on the Privacy page.
- No ads on the IEP and differentiation pages, as the privacy page promises, and no Share-to-library button on those two tools.
- No account, and the documents are kept in my own browser, not on the site.
- The IEP goals are built from the numbers I gave. Each has a condition, an observable behavior, a criterion and a measurement method. The money goal (40% to 80%) and the out-of-seat goal (8 down to 2, with a break card as the replacement) are reasonable starting points. The progress-monitoring section is practical.
- The tool is called "IEP Goal Ideas" and says "to refine with your team". That is the right framing.
- The differentiation document listened: it used my two paraprofessionals, planned for AAC, and where I said no one was ready for extension it wrote "No student in this class is ready for grade-level extension" and offered small next steps instead. The three levels (picture and point, word and sentence, short paragraph) are close to what I would actually run, and the yes/not-yet rubric is usable.
- The text leveler kept every fact from my passage correct (20,000 to 60,000 bees, up to 1,500 eggs, the waggle dance, the causes of colony loss).
- It is fast: 5 to 15 seconds each.
- The library worksheet I opened was correct, with sensible teacher notes (accepting reversed numerals if the quantity is right).

## What I could not check

- How anything looks, prints or reads on a phone; whether the answer key really prints on its own sheet.
- The Print, Word, Copy, Edit, Large print and Revise buttons.
- Whether My documents really stays only in the browser (the page needs JavaScript, which my view did not run), and whether a document with a name in it can be deleted from there.
- What the AI provider does with what is typed.
- Whether the site reacts differently to a full name, a student ID or a diagnosis; I only tried one invented first name.

## What I would say to a colleague about this site

Use the differentiation tool tonight; it gave me a usable three-level plan for my actual class in fifteen seconds. The text leveler is fine for grade 3 and up but check it before you hand it to your lowest readers. For IEP goals, treat it as a brainstorm only: add the "by when", throw out any goal for something the student can already do, and rewrite the objectives. And whatever you do, type "the student" - the site will happily print a name right in the title if you slip.
